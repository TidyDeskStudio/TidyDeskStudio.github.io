How to Track Sponsored Posts and Brand Deals So Nothing Slips Through

The first brand deal feels simple. By the fifth, you have three drafts waiting for approval, one invoice nobody has answered, a post due Thursday and an email thread you cannot find. Missed deadlines and unpaid invoices are rarely about talent; they are about having no single place where every deal lives.

A spreadsheet is enough. Here is how to set one up.

1. One row per deal

Make a tab called Deals. Columns: brand or company, contact name and email, campaign name, stage, agreed fee, date agreed, deliverables (short text), and a link to the contract or email thread. Use invented-looking, plain names so you can read it at a glance.

2. Give every deal a stage

Use a dropdown (Data validation in both Excel and Google Sheets) with a short list: Pitched, Negotiating, Agreed, In production, Awaiting approval, Posted, Invoiced, Paid. A filter on this column turns your sheet into a to-do list. Anything sitting at Invoiced for a long time is your chase list.

3. Track deliverables separately

A single deal often means several pieces: two videos, three stories, a link in your bio for a week. Make a Deliverables tab with one row per piece: deal, what it is, platform, draft due, brand approval date, post date, status. Now your calendar is just this tab sorted by date.

4. Record usage terms and exclusivity

Add columns for how long the brand can reuse your content, and any period where you agreed not to work with a competitor. It is easy to forget these, and easy to check in a spreadsheet before you accept the next offer. A note like "no competing skincare until 30 Jan" saves an awkward conversation later.

5. Payment tracking

On the Deals tab, add: invoice date, payment terms in days, due date, amount paid and date paid. Calculate the due date and flag late ones:

=H2+I2 for due date (invoice date plus terms)

=IF(AND(L2="",TODAY()>J2),"Overdue","") for an overdue flag, where L is the paid date and J the due date.

Both formulas work in Excel and Google Sheets.

6. A simple summary

Use SUMIFS to total fees by month, by stage or by brand. Two numbers matter most: what is agreed but not yet paid, and what you have earned so far this year. Keep it as a record of your own income for your own planning.

Worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Brand: Fernhill Tea Co. Fee agreed: 600. Deliverables: one video and two stories.
  • Draft due 10th, post date 15th, invoice sent 16th with 30-day terms, so payment is due the 16th of next month.
  • The sheet shows the deal at stage Invoiced and, once the due date passes with no paid date, an Overdue flag.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving terms in email only. Copy the key terms into the sheet: fee, dates, usage, exclusivity.
  • Forgetting to invoice. Make Posted automatically mean "invoice now" by filtering on it every Friday.
  • Mixing up gross and net. Note whether the fee is before or after any agency or platform cut.
  • No record of approvals. Put the approval date in the deliverables tab so you can show when something was signed off.

This is a record-keeping method, not tax or legal advice. Check the rules where you live for reporting income and for any disclosure requirements on sponsored content.
