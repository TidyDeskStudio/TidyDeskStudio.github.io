How to Track Tutoring Lessons and Payments So Every Session Is Paid For

Private tutoring is friendly work, which makes money conversations awkward. A parent says they will pay next week, a student cancels twice, someone buys a block of ten lessons and nobody remembers how many are left. A tidy log removes the guesswork, and with it the uncomfortable questions.

This layout works for one tutor with a dozen students or a small team.

Start with a student list

One row per student: name, subject, parent or payer name, contact, hourly rate, lesson length, and a payment style (per lesson, monthly, or package). Add a status column so finished students drop out of dropdowns without being deleted.

Log every lesson as it happens

On a second tab, one row per scheduled lesson: date, student, start time, length in minutes, status, rate, amount due, and notes. Status is the important column. Use a fixed set: Held, Cancelled by student, Cancelled by tutor, No-show. Your cancellation policy decides which of these are billable, so the status drives the money.

Amount due can be a formula: =IF(OR(E2="Held",E2="No-show"),D2/60*F2,0) where D is minutes, E is status and F is the hourly rate. Change the OR list to match your own policy.

Record payments separately

A third tab holds payments: date received, student or payer, amount, method, and what it covered. Keeping payments apart from lessons is what lets you handle part-payments, advance payments and packages without confusion.

The balance formula

On the student list add:

=SUMIF(Lessons!B:B,A2,Lessons!G:G)-SUMIF(Payments!B:B,A2,Payments!C:C)

Positive means they owe you. Negative means they are ahead, for example after buying a package. Apply conditional formatting: red above zero, green at zero or below. A look at that column before each Friday tells you who needs a gentle reminder.

Handling packages

If you sell a block of lessons, record the payment once and track the lessons used. Add "Package lessons bought" to the student list, count held lessons with COUNTIFS, and subtract to show lessons left. Then you can say "you have three lessons remaining" without checking old messages.

Worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Student: Anya Pell, maths, 45-minute lessons at 40 per hour, so 30 per lesson.
  • In October she has 4 held lessons and 1 no-show billed under your policy: 5 x 30 = 150 due.
  • She has paid 120 so far.

Her balance shows 30 owing. A short message with the date and amount settles it, with no need to argue over memory.

Common mistakes

  • Writing the policy down nowhere. Send it to every new family in writing: notice period, what is billed, how to pay.
  • Logging lessons from memory at month end. Fill the status the same day.
  • Using one tab for everything. It becomes impossible to handle advance payments.
  • Chasing late payments only when you feel brave. Pick a routine day instead, so it's business and not personal.
  • No record of lesson content. A notes column with the topic covered helps you plan, and helps if a parent asks what you did.

Keep income records for the year

Payments received by month give you a clean income total when the year ends. This is record keeping, not tax advice; check local rules about reporting self-employment income.
