How to Track Your Net Worth Month by Month (A Simple 10-Minute Routine)

You check your bank balance often. You probably glance at the card statement when it arrives. But if someone asked "are you better off than you were in January?", most of us would have to guess. The money is spread across a current account, savings, maybe a retirement plan, a car loan and a card, and no single statement shows the whole picture.

Net worth is that whole picture: everything you own minus everything you owe. Tracking it once a month is one of the simplest money habits there is, and the end of the year is a good moment to start, because it gives you a clean baseline for next year.

1. List your accounts once

Make an Accounts tab with one row per account or debt:

  • Account - a name you'll recognise, like "Joint savings" or "Car loan"
  • Type - Cash, Savings, Investments, Retirement, Property, Loan, Credit card, Mortgage
  • Asset or Debt
  • Owner - useful for couples who want to see each person's share
  • Held at - where it lives, so you know where to look next month

Be honest about what to include. Things with a clear value you could check, like accounts, investments and your home, belong here. Everyday belongings like furniture usually don't, because their resale value is hard to pin down and doesn't change your decisions.

2. Log balances on the same day each month

Add a Balances tab with three columns: Date, Account, Balance. On the 1st of each month (or whatever day suits you), add a row per account.

Here's a tip that saves a lot of confusion: type debts as positive numbers, exactly as the statement shows them, and let the formulas subtract them. Mixing plus and minus signs by hand is the most common way these sheets go wrong.

3. Let the formulas add it up

Add a helper column in Balances that looks up whether each row is an asset or a debt:

=VLOOKUP(B2,Accounts!A:C,3,FALSE)

Then, on a Summary tab with a list of months in column A:

  • Assets: =SUMIFS(Balances!C:C,Balances!A:A,A2,Balances!D:D,"Asset")
  • Debts: =SUMIFS(Balances!C:C,Balances!A:A,A2,Balances!D:D,"Debt")
  • Net worth: =B2-C2
  • Change since last month: =D3-D2

Make sure the dates in the Balances tab match the months in the Summary exactly (for example, always the 1st), or the totals will come back empty.

4. Look at why it changed

Net worth can move for two different reasons, and it helps to see them separately:

  • Assets went up - you saved, or investments rose in value.
  • Debts went down - you paid off part of a loan or card.

Work out each one (this month's assets minus last month's, and last month's debts minus this month's). A month where markets fell but you paid down a card can still be a good month for your habits, even if the headline number dipped.

A worked example

Example with invented numbers. On 1 October a household has 4,200 in current and savings accounts and 18,500 in a retirement plan, so assets are 22,700. They owe 6,800 on a car loan and 1,150 on a card, so debts are 7,950. Net worth is 14,750.

On 1 November savings are 4,600, the retirement plan is 18,300, the car loan is 6,550 and the card is 700. Assets are 22,900 (up 200) and debts are 7,250 (down 700). Net worth is 15,650, up 900. Most of the progress came from paying debt down, even though the retirement balance slipped a little.

5. Add one or two goals

A Goals tab keeps the routine motivating: Goal, Account, Target, Target date, Current. For a savings goal, progress is =MIN(1,E2/C2). For paying a card to zero, compare today's balance with where it started. Keep it to a couple of goals so the sheet stays quick to update.

Common mistakes

  • Updating at random times. Balances swing around payday. Pick one date and stick to it.
  • Forgetting a debt. A store card or a buy-now-pay-later plan left off the list makes the picture rosier than it is.
  • Overvaluing things. If you include your home or car, use a cautious estimate and update it rarely.
  • Reacting to one month. Investment balances move. Look at the trend over six or twelve months.
  • Making it a chore. If the update takes an hour, you'll stop. Keep the account list short and the routine to ten minutes.

This is a record-keeping method for your own numbers, not financial advice.

Ten minutes on the 1st

Open each account, type the balances in, look at the change. After a few months you'll have a real answer to "are we getting ahead?", which is more useful than any single bank balance.
