Most chore charts start well and fade within a fortnight. The bins are missed, someone says it was not their turn, and pocket money turns into a debate nobody can settle because nobody wrote anything down. The fix is not a prettier chart. It is a system that makes the facts easy to see.

Here is a points-based rota you can build in a spreadsheet.

Step 1: List the jobs and give each a points value

Write every regular job in one list: emptying the dishwasher, feeding the pet, taking out bins, tidying a bedroom, vacuuming the lounge. Then give each one a points value based on effort and time, not on how much anyone likes it. Something like:

  • 5 points for quick jobs (feed the pet).
  • 10 points for medium jobs (empty the dishwasher).
  • 20 points for bigger jobs (vacuum the house).

Agree the values together. Children accept a system they helped set far more easily than one handed down.

Step 2: Decide who does what, and when

Make a weekly grid: jobs down the side, days across the top, and a name in each cell. Rotate the less popular jobs so no one has them permanently. Keep a column for due day, so everyone can see what is expected and when.

Step 3: Tick it off in one place

Add a done column or a tick-box per day. The key is that it is in one shared place that everybody can see. A tick is a fact. A memory of who did it is an argument.

Step 4: Add up points per person

For each person, total points for the week with a conditional sum:

=SUMIF(B2:B40,"Sam",E2:E40)

Here column B holds the name and column E the points for jobs that are ticked off. Change the name for each person and you have a weekly score.

Step 5: Turn points into pocket money

Pick a rate, for example 1 point equals a small fixed amount, or a base amount plus a bonus for beating a target. Then:

=Points*Rate

If you prefer a base allowance, set a minimum points target, and only pay the bonus if it is met. This keeps pocket money tied to something visible instead of to mood.

A worked example

Invented numbers, for layout only.

  • Rate: 0.10 per point. Weekly target: 60 points.
  • Sam completes 70 points, so earns 7.00, with a bonus because the target is met.
  • Alex completes 45 points and earns 4.50, with no bonus. The sheet shows exactly which jobs were missed, so the conversation is about those jobs, not about fairness in general.

Add rewards and streaks if they help

Some families like a bigger reward for a long streak, such as a week with every job done. Count it with a simple column that shows consecutive complete weeks. Keep it light. If a reward system becomes a source of stress, simplify it.

Common mistakes

  • Giving a job no points value. Unpaid jobs get skipped.
  • Changing values mid-week. Agree changes at the weekly review.
  • Having no way to see who did what. That is where the arguments start.
  • Making it too big. Start with five or six jobs, not twenty.
  • Docking points as punishment. Decide your own approach, but keep the record honest.

This is a household record-keeping tool, not parenting or financial advice. Adjust it to suit your family and your children's ages.
