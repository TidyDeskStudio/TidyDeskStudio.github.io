Why Your Online Shop Payout Doesn't Match Your Sales (And How to Reconcile It)

Your sales report says you made 1,240 last week. Your bank shows 1,052. Somewhere between those two numbers sit fees, refunds, a held payout, maybe an error. If you sell on more than one channel, the gap gets harder to explain every month.

Reconciling means matching what you should have received to what you did receive, one payout at a time. It is a short routine once the sheet is set up.

Why the numbers never match by default

  • Channels deduct fees before paying you, so the deposit is smaller than gross sales.
  • Refunds and chargebacks reduce a later payout, not the one for the original sale.
  • Payouts arrive on a schedule, so a sale made today may land next week.
  • Other deductions sneak in: shipping labels, advertising, subscription charges.

None of this is a mystery, but it is only visible if you record each piece separately.

Set up one row per payout statement

Make a Payouts tab. Each time a channel sends you a statement, add a row with these columns:

  • Payout date
  • Channel (drop-down from a small list of your channels)
  • Reference (the statement or batch number)
  • Gross sales
  • Refunds
  • Fees charged
  • Other deductions
  • Received in bank (leave empty until it arrives)

Add the expected and variance columns

With gross in D, refunds in E, fees in F, other deductions in G and received in H:

  • Expected payout: =D2-E2-F2-G2
  • Variance: =IF(H2="","",H2-I2)
  • Check: =IF(H2="","Awaiting payout",IF(ABS(J2)<=1,"Matched",IF(J2<0,"Short","Over")))

The 1 is a tolerance so rounding pennies do not clutter your list. Change it to suit. Filter the check column for Short and Awaiting payout, and that is your to-do list.

Check the fee rate too

Add a Channels tab with each channel's expected fee rate, for example 6.5%. Then add a column on Payouts: =F2/D2 for the actual fee rate, and compare it to the expected one. If the real rate is higher, there may be extra charges worth reading about on the statement. This catches slow creep that no single payout reveals.

A worked example

All invented. A shop sells on two channels. Channel A reports 600 gross, 20 refunds and 39 in fees (6.5%), so you expect 541. The bank shows 541. Matched. Channel B reports 640 gross, 0 refunds, 51.20 fees (8%) and 15 in other deductions, so you expect 573.80. The bank shows 558.80. That is Short by exactly 15, so you open the statement and find a deduction that was not listed in the summary. You write to the channel with the reference number and the amount. Without the sheet, a 15 gap hides inside a month of deposits.

Totals by month

A Monthly tab turns the log into a picture. Use =SUMPRODUCT((MONTH(A2:A600)=1)*(D2:D600)) for gross in January, and the same pattern for fees, refunds and received. Divide fees by gross to see your real cost of selling, month by month, and compare channels honestly.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing to sales dates instead of payout dates. Reconcile by statement.
  • Skipping the reference number. You will need it when you contact support.
  • Ignoring small shortfalls. They add up and repeat.
  • Leaving payouts to the end of the quarter. Disputes are easier while the statement is recent.

This is a record-keeping method, not accounting or tax advice. Ask a qualified bookkeeper if you are unsure how to treat fees and refunds in your accounts.
