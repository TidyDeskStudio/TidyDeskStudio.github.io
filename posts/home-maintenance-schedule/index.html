How to Make a Home Maintenance Schedule You'll Actually Keep (Plus What to Check Before Winter)

Most homeowners know the gutters need clearing and the smoke alarms need testing. The trouble is remembering when you last did them. Was the furnace serviced last autumn or the one before? Is the washing machine still under warranty? Without a record, jobs slip until something leaks, stops working or costs three times as much to fix.

A maintenance schedule fixes that. It doesn't need to be fancy. It needs three things: a list of regular jobs, the date each was last done, and a way to see what's coming up. Here's how to set one up, with a short pre-winter list to get you started.

1. List the jobs that repeat

Walk around the house, room by room and then outside, and write down every job that comes back on a cycle. Give each one a row with these columns:

  • Task - short and specific, like "Replace furnace filter" rather than "Heating"
  • Area - Kitchen, Bathroom, Heating & cooling, Plumbing, Electrical, Roof & gutters, Garden, Exterior, Appliances, Safety
  • Every (months) - how far apart it should happen
  • Last done - the date, even if it's a rough guess for now
  • Est. cost - zero for jobs you do yourself
  • Notes - filter size, which ladder, who to call

For how often, use the manual or the installer's advice where you have it. Where you don't, pick a sensible interval and adjust it once you see how things wear in your home.

2. Let the sheet work out the next due date

This is the part that makes the schedule run itself. With Every (months) in C and Last done in D:

=IF(OR(D2="",C2=""),"",EDATE(D2,C2))

Format that column as a date. Then count the days left with =IF(G2="","",G2-TODAY()), where G is the next due date. When you finish a job, you only change one cell: Last done.

3. Add a status so the urgent jobs stand out

Put a warning window in a settings cell, say 30 days in B1. Then a status column:

=IF(A2="","",IF(G2="","Set date",IF(H2<0,"Overdue",IF(H2<=$B$1,"Due soon","OK"))))

Add conditional formatting so Overdue is red and Due soon is amber. Sort or filter by status once a week and you'll know exactly what needs doing this weekend.

4. Keep a repair log next to it

Scheduled jobs are only half the picture. Give one-off repairs their own tab: Date, Area, Job, Contractor, Cost, Covered until (the date the tradesperson's warranty on the work ends). It pays off when the same tap leaks again and you want to know if the fix is still covered, or when you sell and a buyer asks what work has been done.

A third tab for contractors (name, trade, phone, a 1-5 rating) means you're not searching old messages for the plumber you liked. A fourth for appliances (bought date, warranty end date) lets you add a simple alert: =IF(F2="","",IF(F2.

What to check before winter

October is a good month to update your list, because a lot of jobs are cheaper to do before the cold arrives:

  • Clear gutters and downpipes once most of the leaves are down
  • Book the boiler or furnace service before engineers get busy
  • Replace the heating filter
  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
  • Check outside taps and pipes that could freeze, and know where your stopcock is
  • Look for gaps around doors and windows
  • Clean the dryer vent
  • Put the garden furniture and mower away clean

A worked example (invented numbers)

Say you last cleaned the gutters on 15 May and you do it every 6 months. EDATE gives a next due date of 15 November. On 20 October that's 26 days away, inside the 30-day window, so the status reads Due soon. The furnace was serviced on 10 November last year on a 12-month cycle, so it's due on 10 November too and also shows Due soon. Two jobs, estimated at 90 and 120, show up together, so you can book both this month instead of finding out in December.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving Last done blank. A guess is better than nothing. The status will say "Set date" until you fill it in.
  • Vague task names. "Check bathroom" never gets done. "Reseal shower edges" does.
  • Not updating after the job. Change the date the moment you finish, or the job will keep showing as overdue.
  • Throwing away receipts. Note the cost and cover end date in the repair log, and keep a photo of the receipt.
