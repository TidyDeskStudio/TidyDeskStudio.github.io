A bride messages you on Tuesday, pays a deposit on Wednesday, books a trial for the following month, and adds two bridesmaids on Friday. By the weekend that one wedding lives in your DMs, your bank app, your calendar and a notes page. Then a second enquiry arrives for the same Saturday and you can't remember if you're still free.

Makeup bookings go wrong in small, predictable ways: a missing deposit, a double-booked date, a balance nobody chased. One sheet fixes most of it. Here is how to set it up.

Start with one row per booking

Don't give every appointment its own tab. Use one row per booking, where a booking is one client and one event. Weddings with a trial and a wedding day are still one row; the extra details live in columns.

Useful columns:

  • Client name and contact
  • Event type (bridal, bridesmaids, prom, photoshoot, event)
  • Event date and start time
  • Location and travel time
  • People (how many faces)
  • Total price
  • Deposit due and deposit received (date)
  • Balance due date and balance received
  • Status (enquiry, held, confirmed, completed, cancelled)

Treat the deposit as the thing that confirms the date

A date isn't booked until the deposit is in. Put that rule in your status column: held means you're keeping the date for a few days, confirmed means the deposit has arrived. Add a column called Hold expires so a held date doesn't block your calendar forever.

A formula keeps you honest. If your deposit-received column is G and your status is H, this flags rows that say confirmed but have no deposit:

=IF(AND(H2="Confirmed",G2=""),"Check deposit","")

Give trials their own columns

A trial is a separate appointment, often weeks before the wedding, and it's easy to lose. Add Trial date, Trial fee and Trial done? to the same row. Then sort by trial date and you can see next month's trials at a glance. Record what was decided too: lip shade, lash style, skin notes and any allergies mentioned, so the wedding morning isn't a guess.

Calculate the balance automatically

Don't do sums in your head. With total price in column F, trial fee in J and deposit received amount in K:

=F2+J2-K2

gives the amount still owed. Add a column that turns red when the balance due date is within seven days and nothing has been received. In Excel or Google Sheets that's a conditional formatting rule using =AND(M2-TODAY()<=7,N2="").

Check for clashes before you say yes

Before you confirm a date, filter or sort the sheet by event date. Count what's already on it with:

=COUNTIF(C:C,DATE(2027,6,12))

If the result is 1 or more, check the start times and travel before agreeing. Add a column for Finish time (start plus number of people times the minutes you allow each, plus travel) so back-to-back events don't surprise you.

A worked example (invented numbers)

Say Priya Example books bridal makeup for 12 June plus three bridesmaids.

  • Total price: 420 (bride 150, three bridesmaids at 70 each, plus 60 travel)
  • Deposit: 100, received 3 February
  • Trial: 14 May, fee 50, paid on the day
  • Balance due: 29 May

Balance = 420 + 50 - 100 - 50 = 320. The sheet shows 320 owed until the balance lands. Status moves from held, to confirmed on 3 February, to completed after the day. Nothing is held in your memory.

Common mistakes

  • Holding dates without an expiry. A date held forever blocks real clients.
  • Mixing deposits and fees. Keep trial fees, deposits and balances in separate columns so you can tell what's what.
  • Skipping cancellations. Keep cancelled rows with a status of cancelled and a note about the deposit policy you agreed, instead of deleting them.
  • Not recording the agreed terms. Put your cancellation and deposit terms in writing with each client and note the date you sent them.

This is a record-keeping method, not legal or tax advice. Check local rules for anything about contracts or deposits.
