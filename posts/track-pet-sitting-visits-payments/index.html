How to Keep Track of Pet Sitting Visits, Holiday Bookings and Who Has Paid

Pet sitting gets hectic around school breaks and holidays. You have drop-in visits, overnight stays, a few daily dog walks and a spare key to somebody's house. Meanwhile you need to remember who has paid, who owes for last week and what the holiday surcharge was. Texts and a paper diary stop working at about the fifth client.

Here is a layout that holds up when you are busy.

Client and pet list

One row per pet, linked to an owner: owner name, phone, address, pet name and type, vet contact, feeding and medication notes, key or access details (keep these sensible and private), and emergency contact. You will open this tab on the doorstep more than any other, so keep it readable.

A rate table

Put your prices in a small table instead of typing them every time: 30-minute visit, 60-minute visit, dog walk, overnight stay, extra pet fee, holiday surcharge. When a rate changes, you edit one cell and new bookings use it. Name the rates plainly, for example "Visit 30".

The visit log

One row per visit: date, owner, pet, service, number of extra pets, holiday (yes or no), amount, paid (yes or no), and notes. The amount can be calculated so you can't forget the surcharge:

=VLOOKUP(D2,Rates!A:B,2,FALSE)+E2*Rates!B8+IF(F2="Yes",Rates!B9,0)

Here D is the service, E the extra pets, F the holiday flag and Rates rows 8 and 9 hold the extra-pet fee and holiday surcharge. VLOOKUP works in both Excel and Google Sheets, so you can move files between them.

Handling multi-day bookings

For a week away, enter one row per visit day, or one row for the stay with a number of nights. Pick whichever you can keep up with consistently. One row per day makes the log match your actual workload, and it's easy to copy a row down.

Show unpaid balances

On a summary tab, list each owner with:

=SUMIFS(Visits!H:H,Visits!B:B,A2,Visits!I:I,"No")

That total is what the owner still owes. Sort by largest, and send a friendly reminder once a week. Add a payment date column to the log if you'd like to see how long people usually take.

Worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Owner: Priya Lund, one dog (Biscuit) and one cat.
  • Three 30-minute visits at 18, one extra pet fee of 5 per visit, one visit on a holiday with a surcharge of 8.
  • Total = 3 x 18 + 3 x 5 + 8 = 77.
  • Priya paid 40 upfront, so 37 is outstanding.

You can send the exact breakdown, which keeps the conversation friendly because it is just arithmetic.

Plan the busy weeks early

Add a calendar-style tab or a filter by date to see how many visits are booked on each day. If you are already stretched on the days around a holiday, you will see it before you accept another booking, not after.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the extras. Extra pets, medication and holiday rates are the usual leaks.
  • Paper notes for pet care. A missed medication detail matters more than a missed payment.
  • No cancellation terms. Put them in writing and note exceptions in the log.
  • Mixing personal and business payments. Record the payment method so it's easy to reconcile later.

This is a record-keeping method, not tax or legal advice; check your local rules for insurance, licensing and reporting income.
