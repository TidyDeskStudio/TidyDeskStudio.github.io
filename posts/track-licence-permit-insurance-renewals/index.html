How to Track Licence, Permit and Insurance Renewal Dates So Nothing Lapses

If you work in a trade or run a small service business, you probably hold several documents that expire: a trade licence, a local permit, a vehicle or liability policy, maybe a certification. Each one renews on its own date, with its own office, and none of them sends a reminder you can rely on.

One missed renewal can mean a gap in cover or a pause in work. The cure is a single register you check on a schedule.

Step 1: List everything that can expire

Go through your folders, emails and wallet. Write down every licence, permit, certificate and insurance policy. Don't judge which are important yet. If it has an end date, it goes on the list.

Step 2: Give each item the same columns

  • Item name
  • Type (licence, permit, insurance, certification)
  • Issued by (the office or insurer)
  • Reference number
  • Expiry date
  • Renewal fee (estimate)
  • Who handles it
  • Where the document is kept

Don't store passwords or login details in this sheet. A note such as "online account, login in password manager" is enough.

Step 3: Calculate the days left

In a new column, subtract today from the expiry date:

=E2-TODAY()

Format the result as a number, not a date. A negative number means the item has already expired.

Step 4: Turn the number into a status

Use one formula to give each row a plain-word status:

=IF(F2<0,"Expired",IF(F2<=30,"Renew now",IF(F2<=90,"Coming up","OK")))

Then sort or filter by the days-left column so the nearest dates sit at the top. Add conditional formatting if you like, red for Expired, amber for Renew now, but the words alone work.

Choose your own thresholds. Some renewals take weeks of paperwork, so those might need a 90-day warning. A simple online renewal may need only 14.

Step 5: Plan the cost, not only the date

Renewals tend to cluster, and a month with three fees can hurt. Add up fees by month:

=SUMPRODUCT((MONTH(E2:E30)=11)*(YEAR(E2:E30)=2027)*G2:G30)

Change the month number for each month of the year, and you can see where the heavy months are and put money aside in the lighter ones.

A worked example

All names and numbers invented, for layout only.

  • Trade licence, expires 14 March, fee 160, days left 40 as of 2 February: Coming up.
  • Van insurance, expires 21 February, fee 410, days left 19: Renew now.
  • Site permit, expired 30 January, fee 75: Expired, so it goes to the top of today's to-do list.
  • March total: 160. February total: 410.

From one glance you know what to do today and where the next big bill lands.

Step 6: Set a review habit

Open the sheet on the first of every month. Look at anything Renew now or Coming up, assign it, and record when the renewal is submitted. When you receive the new document, update the expiry date and file the copy. Ten minutes once a month is enough.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting reminder emails. They go to spam, to an old address, or never arrive.
  • Tracking only insurance. Permits and certifications are just as easy to miss.
  • Not recording the renewal as done. Next year's date should replace the old one the same day.
  • Leaving long-lead renewals late. Check how long each office takes to process.

This is a record-keeping method, not legal advice. Requirements differ by place and trade, so check with the issuing office what you need and when.
