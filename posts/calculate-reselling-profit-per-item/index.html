How to Calculate Your Real Profit on Each Item You Resell (After Fees and Shipping)

You bought a jacket for 8 and sold it for 48. That feels like 40 profit. Then the selling fee comes off, you pay for postage and a mailer, and you drove to three thrift stores to find it. The real number is smaller, and on some items it's close to nothing.

Most resellers know this in theory. The problem is that nobody does the maths item by item, so the slow sellers and the low earners hide behind a few good flips. A simple tracker shows you what each item really made, and it's worth setting one up before the busy end-of-year selling season.

1. Give every item one row, from purchase to sale

The key idea is that one row follows one item for its whole life. Fill in the first columns when you buy it and the rest as it moves along:

  • SKU - a short code you also write on the tag or bag, like A014
  • Item, Category and Source (thrift store, garage sale, estate sale, clearance, online, wholesale lot)
  • Buy date and Cost
  • Channel and List price - filled in when you list it
  • Sold date, Sale price, Fees and Shipping - filled in when it sells

Use dropdowns for Category, Source and Channel so the totals group properly later.

2. Work out profit per item

With cost in F, sale price in J, fees in K and shipping in L:

=IF(J2="","",J2-SUM(F2,K2,L2))

SUM ignores blank cells, so a local pickup sale with no fees or postage still works. Put in the shipping you actually paid. If the buyer paid postage to you, include that in the sale price so the two balance out.

3. Add return on cost and days to sell

Profit alone doesn't tell you which finds are worth chasing. Two more columns help:

  • ROI (profit as a share of what you paid): =IF(OR(M2="",F2=0),"",M2/F2), formatted as a percentage
  • Days to sell: =IF(OR(I2="",E2=""),"",I2-E2)

A 30 profit that took four days is a different deal from a 30 profit that took four months. Seeing both helps you decide what to buy next time.

4. Watch the stock that isn't moving

Add a Status column: =IF(B2="","",IF(I2<>"","Sold",IF(G2<>"","Listed","Not listed"))). Then a Days in stock column for unsold items: =IF(OR(B2="",I2<>""),"",TODAY()-E2).

Sort by days in stock every few weeks. Anything that's been sitting for a long time needs new photos, a lower price, a different channel or a bundle. Also add up the cost of everything not yet sold. That's cash you've spent but haven't got back yet.

5. Log the costs that aren't tied to one item

Mailers, tape, fuel for sourcing trips, storage, listing tools and a clothing rack don't belong to any single item, but they still come out of your profit. Keep a small Expenses tab with Date, Type, Description and Amount. Your real result for the year is total item profit minus total expenses.

A worked example (invented numbers)

A wool coat bought for 14 sells on your own site for 78. Fees are 2.60 and shipping is 12, so profit is 78 - 14 - 2.60 - 12 = 49.40, an ROI of about 353%, and it took 41 days to sell. A film camera bought for 15 sells for 110 on a marketplace with 14.30 in fees and 11 shipping: profit 69.70, ROI about 465%, sold in 25 days. Meanwhile a speaker bought for 22 has been listed for a month with no sale. Add 40 of supplies and subscriptions for the month, and you can see what the month really earned and which find needs attention.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting the sale price as income and forgetting fees and postage.
  • Not logging an item until it sells. Then you never see what's sitting unsold.
  • Leaving out supplies and fuel. Small costs add up over a year.
  • Different spellings for the same channel. Use a dropdown so your totals by channel are right.

This is about keeping clear records. It isn't tax advice. How resale income is taxed depends on where you live, so check your local rules or ask an accountant.
