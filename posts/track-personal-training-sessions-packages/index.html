How to Track Personal Training Sessions and Prepaid Packages Without Losing Count

"How many sessions do I have left?" is the question every personal trainer hears, usually right when you can't remember. If you sell blocks of sessions, you are also running a small prepaid balance for every client. A sheet that counts the sessions down for you takes about twenty minutes to set up and saves a lot of awkward guessing.

Three tabs are enough

Clients: name, contact, start date, goal, the package they are on, and notes such as injuries to remember. Sessions: one row per booked session. Payments: one row per payment received. Keeping them apart means a client can buy a second package without breaking anything.

The sessions tab

Columns: date, client, time, length, session type (one-to-one, partner, online), status, and a short note. Use a dropdown for status with a small fixed list:

  • Completed
  • Late cancel (inside your notice window)
  • Cancelled in time
  • No-show

Decide in advance which of these use up a session from the package. Many trainers count Completed, Late cancel and No-show, and give back Cancelled in time. Whatever your rule is, write it on the client's agreement and make the sheet follow it.

Count sessions used and sessions left

On the Clients tab, with the package size in column E and the client name in column A:

=COUNTIFS(Sessions!B:B,A2,Sessions!F:F,"Completed")+COUNTIFS(Sessions!B:B,A2,Sessions!F:F,"Late cancel")+COUNTIFS(Sessions!B:B,A2,Sessions!F:F,"No-show")

That is sessions used. Sessions remaining is simply =E2-F2. Add conditional formatting so a client with two sessions or fewer turns amber. That's your cue to mention renewing before they run out, not after.

Package expiry

If packages expire, add a purchase date and an expiry column: =B2+90 for a 90-day window. A formula such as =IF(AND(G2>0,TODAY()>H2),"Expired","") flags clients with unused sessions past their date, so you can decide what to do under your own policy before it becomes an argument.

Worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Client: Theo Marsh, package of 10 sessions, bought 1 October, 90-day window.
  • By 24 October: 6 Completed, 1 Late cancel, 1 Cancelled in time.
  • Used = 7 (Completed plus Late cancel). Remaining = 3.
  • Expiry = 30 December, so there's plenty of time, and the amber flag appears because only 3 remain.

A message like "you have three sessions left, shall I pencil in the next ones?" is easy to send when the number is right there.

Track money separately from sessions

On Payments, record date, client, amount and method. Then the client balance is =SUMIF(Payments!B:B,A2,Payments!C:C) for total paid. For monthly income, a SUMIF on the month gives you what came in without hunting through bank messages.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping counts in your head or on a paper card. One lost card and the numbers are a dispute.
  • Not recording cancellations. The late-cancel pattern is useful to see.
  • Vague notice rules. A short written policy protects both of you.
  • Forgetting renewals. Flag low balances weekly.

This is a record-keeping method, not tax, legal or health advice; check your local rules for business records and client health information.
