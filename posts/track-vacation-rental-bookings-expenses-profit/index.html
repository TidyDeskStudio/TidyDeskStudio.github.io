How to Track Vacation Rental Bookings and Expenses to See Your Real Profit

A full calendar feels like success. But a booked month and a profitable month are different things once you subtract cleaning, supplies, platform fees, utilities and the repairs nobody planned for. Many hosts only discover which stays earned money when they sit down at year end.

A simple two-tab sheet shows it as you go.

Tab one: the bookings log

One row per stay. Columns that earn their place:

  • Guest name (invented or initials are fine for your own use), booking source, check-in, check-out
  • Nights, nightly rate, cleaning fee charged, total paid by guest
  • Platform fee, payout received, payout date

Nights come from a formula: =C2-B2 where B is check-in and C is check-out. Do not type it by hand; a typo here ruins every average later.

Tab two: the expense log

One row per expense: date, category, description, amount, and which property if you have more than one. Keep the category list short and fixed: Cleaning, Supplies, Utilities, Repairs, Furnishing, Software and fees, Insurance, Other. A dropdown prevents "cleaner", "Cleaning" and "cleaning service" turning into three categories.

The four numbers worth watching

Occupancy rate is the share of available nights that were booked. For a month: =SUMIFS(Bookings!D:D,Bookings!B:B,">="&A2,Bookings!B:B,"<"&EDATE(A2,1))/DAY(EOMONTH(A2,0)) where A2 is the first day of the month. It is simple but counts nights by check-in month, so a stay that crosses a month boundary lands in the month it began. That is fine for tracking trends.

Average nightly rate: =SUM(room revenue)/SUM(nights).

Expenses by category: =SUMIFS(Expenses!D:D,Expenses!B:B,"Cleaning"), one cell per category.

Profit: payouts received minus expenses for the same period. Put the two side by side on a summary tab by month.

Worked example

Invented numbers.

  • July: 18 nights booked out of 31, so occupancy is about 58 percent.
  • Payouts received: 2,340. Expenses: cleaning 360, supplies 85, utilities 140, repairs 120.
  • Profit for July: 2,340 minus 705 = 1,635.

Look at the average per booked night, 1,635 divided by 18, about 91. Compare that with a month at lower occupancy but higher rates. Sometimes fewer, pricier nights beat more cheap ones.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the booking total as income. Platform fees come out before the money reaches you. Track the payout.
  • Forgetting cleaning fee costs. The fee you charge guests and the amount you pay the cleaner are different numbers.
  • Leaving small purchases out. Coffee pods, toilet paper and light bulbs add up. Photograph receipts and log them weekly.
  • Mixing personal use with rental use. If you stay in the property yourself, record those nights separately.
  • Ignoring one-off costs. A new mattress is a different kind of expense from monthly supplies. Give it its own category so it doesn't distort the monthly view.

Use the numbers for pricing

Once you have a few months of data, look at which weekdays and seasons booked at what rate, and which gaps between stays stayed empty. That is far better evidence for a price change than a hunch.

This is record keeping, not tax advice. Rules on what counts as rental income and expense vary by place, so check local rules or ask an accountant.
