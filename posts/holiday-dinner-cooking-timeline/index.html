How to Make a Holiday Dinner Cooking Timeline (Working Backwards From Serving Time)

Most holiday dinners don't go wrong because of a bad recipe. They go wrong in the last hour. The roast is resting, the potatoes still need forty minutes, the rolls want a hotter oven than the stuffing, and someone asks whether the gravy is happening. Everything is cooked, just not at the same time.

The fix is simple: decide when you want to eat, then work backwards for every dish. Once it's written down, the day turns into a list of times instead of a list of worries.

1. Pick a serving time and stick to it

Choose the exact time food goes on the table, not a rough "late afternoon". Every other time in the plan hangs off this one. If guests tend to run late, build that into the serving time rather than letting it drift on the day.

2. List every dish with three numbers

Make a table with one row per dish and these columns:

  • Dish and Course (starter, main, side, dessert)
  • Prep minutes - peeling, chopping, assembling
  • Cook minutes - time in the oven or on the hob
  • Rest minutes - resting meat, cooling a pie, letting a bake set
  • Method - oven, hob, slow cooker, no-cook
  • Oven temp - only for oven dishes
  • Days ahead - 0 for the day itself, 1 for the day before, and so on

Use the times from your own recipes and add a little slack for anything you haven't made before. Don't forget the small things: gravy, warming plates, whipping cream.

3. Let the formulas count backwards

Put your serving date and time in one cell, say B1, as a full date-time like 26/11/2026 15:00. With prep, cook and rest minutes in columns C, D and E and days ahead in H:

  • Out of oven: =$B$1-H2-E2/1440
  • Into oven: =I2-D2/1440 (where I is "Out of oven")
  • Start prep: =J2-C2/1440 (where J is "Into oven")

There are 1,440 minutes in a day, which is why the minutes are divided by 1440. Format the three result columns with the custom format ddd hh:mm so you see "Thu 13:20" rather than a long decimal. Then sort by Start prep and you have your running order.

4. Check the oven for clashes

Most home kitchens have one oven, and it can only be one temperature. Look down the Into oven and Out of oven times for oven dishes. Any two whose times overlap at different temperatures need a fix: cook one earlier and reheat it, move one to the hob, or pick a middle temperature and adjust the time.

Resting time is your friend here. A roast that rests for 30-45 minutes under foil frees the oven for sides and bread.

5. Move work to the days before

Anything that holds well should leave the big day: cranberry sauce, pie pastry, desserts, chopped veg kept in water or bags in the fridge, a make-ahead stuffing. Set its Days ahead to 1 or 2 and the timeline shows it on the right day.

6. Tie in guests and shopping

Keep a short guest list with RSVP, party size, food preferences and what each person is bringing. A dish someone else brings comes off your timeline but stays on the list so it isn't forgotten. Then build the shopping list from the dishes, grouped by store section, so one trip covers it.

A worked example (invented numbers)

Serving time is Thursday at 15:00. The turkey needs 30 minutes prep, 210 minutes cooking and 40 minutes rest. Counting back: out of the oven at 14:20, into the oven at 10:50, start prep at 10:20. The roast potatoes were par-boiled earlier and need 40 minutes at a higher heat, so they go in at 14:20, the moment the turkey comes out and the oven gets turned up. Rolls need 15 minutes at that same heat and slide in alongside them at 14:40. The pie was baked on Wednesday, so it doesn't touch Thursday's oven at all.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting resting and cooling time. It's free oven time if you plan for it, and a scramble if you don't.
  • Assuming two dishes can share the oven without checking their temperatures.
  • Leaving everything for the day. If it can be made ahead, make it ahead.
  • No buffer for preheating. Add the minutes your oven takes to heat up, especially after turning it up.
  • Keeping the plan in your head. Print the running order and stick it on the fridge.
