How to Keep a Mileage Log for Business Driving (and Catch Up Before Year-End)

If you drive to clients, job sites or the supplier, those trips are a business cost. In many places you can claim them, but only if there's a record. And the record is the part people skip: it's hard to remember in March where you drove on a Tuesday in July.

The good news is that a mileage log is one of the simplest records you can keep. It takes under a minute per trip, and with the year nearly over, now is a good time to start one or fill in the gaps.

This post is about keeping clear records. It isn't tax advice. Rules and approved rates differ between countries and change over time, so check with your local tax authority or an accountant.

1. Decide what one row looks like

Give every trip its own row. These columns cover what's usually asked for:

  • Date
  • Purpose - client visit, site visit, supplies, delivery, training
  • Client / destination - who or where, specific enough that you'd recognise it later
  • Vehicle - if you use more than one
  • Start odometer and End odometer, or Distance (manual) if you look the distance up on a map
  • Round trip? and Business? - Yes or No dropdowns
  • Notes

Use dropdowns for Purpose and Vehicle. Typing "client visit" one day and "Client mtg" the next makes your totals by purpose useless.

2. Let the sheet work out the distance

Some trips you'll have odometer readings for, others you won't. One formula can handle both. With start in E, end in F, manual distance in G and round trip in H:

=IF(AND(G2="",F2=""),"",IF(G2<>"",G2,F2-E2)*IF(H2="Yes",2,1))

If you typed a manual distance it's used, otherwise the odometer difference is. A one-way distance on a round trip is doubled, so you only need to look up the outbound leg.

3. Add a claim value column

Put your claim rate per mile or km in a single settings cell, say B1, and name it so it's easy to find. Then:

=IF(OR(J2="",I2="No"),"",ROUND(J2*$B$1,2))

Personal trips stay in the log but get no claim value. Keeping them in is useful, because some tax authorities ask what share of your total driving was for business.

4. Total it by year, month and purpose

A small summary area answers most questions. Business distance for the year, with dates in A, the Business flag in I and distance in J:

=SUMIFS(J:J,I:I,"Yes",A:A,">="&DATE(2026,1,1),A:A,"<"&DATE(2027,1,1))

Swap the dates for a month range to see each month, or add a Purpose condition to see how much driving goes to deliveries versus client visits. Dividing business distance by total distance gives you the business share.

A worked example (invented numbers)

Say a self-employed decorator uses a rate of 0.70 per mile. On one day they drive 26 miles to a client (odometer 40210 to 40236), then later log a supplier run as 9 miles one way, marked as a round trip, so 18 miles. That's 44 business miles and a claim value of 30.80 for the day. The weekend trip to see family, 210 miles, is logged with Business = No and adds nothing to the claim. At year-end, the summary shows business miles, claim value and business share, without anyone digging through a calendar.

5. Catching up on missing months

If you haven't kept a log this year, don't invent trips. Rebuild what you can prove:

  • Go through your calendar, invoices and job records month by month.
  • Use fuel receipts and card statements to confirm which days you were out.
  • Look up distances with a map and note in the Notes column that they were reconstructed.
  • From today, log trips as they happen.

A reconstructed log based on real records is far better than a round number guessed at tax time. Ask your accountant how your local rules treat it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Logging the commute from home to a regular workplace as business. Check how your local rules treat it.
  • Writing "meeting" with no destination. Name the client or place.
  • Waiting until the end of the month to fill it in. Log the trip before you start the engine for the next one.
  • Forgetting the odometer reading on 1 January. Take a photo of the dashboard each new year.
