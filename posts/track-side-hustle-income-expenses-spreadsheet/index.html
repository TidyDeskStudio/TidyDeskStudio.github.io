How to Keep Track of Side Hustle Income and Expenses From Day One

A side hustle often starts casually. A payment lands in one account, another comes by transfer, and the receipts end up in a drawer or a phone photo album. A few months later you honestly can't say what it made.

The fix is small: one log, filled in as things happen, and a ten-minute check once a month.

Step 1: Log every payment you receive

One row per payment. Columns:

  • Date received
  • Client or source
  • Description
  • Amount
  • Invoice number (if you send them)
  • Status: Paid, Invoiced or Overdue

Log invoiced-but-unpaid work as well, with the status set to Invoiced. That keeps money you are owed from slipping out of sight.

Step 2: Log every cost

On a second tab, one row per expense: date, supplier, category, amount, and a note on where the receipt is kept. Typical categories are supplies, software, travel, fees, equipment and advertising. Choose your own list, but keep it short so you use it consistently.

Snap a photo of each receipt on the day and name it with the date and supplier. Filing later is where most records get lost.

Step 3: Add the monthly totals

Add a helper column with the month, like =TEXT(A2,"yyyy-mm"), then total by month:

=SUMIF(Income!G:G,"2026-11",Income!D:D)

Do the same on the expense tab. Profit is income minus expenses:

=B2-C2

Quarterly totals are just three months added up, which helps if your local rules ask for quarterly figures.

Step 4: Set money aside as you go

If you owe tax on side income where you live, a surprise bill is stressful. Pick a percentage to set aside from each month's profit and put it in its own pot:

=MAX(0,Profit)*SetAsidePercent

The right percentage depends on your situation and where you live, so confirm it with a qualified professional or your local tax authority. The sheet just helps you set the money aside as you earn it.

A worked example

All numbers invented, for layout only.

  • Income in October: 620 from three clients, plus 150 invoiced but not yet paid.
  • Expenses in October: 45 supplies, 20 software, 15 fees. Total 80.
  • Profit: 620 - 80 = 540.
  • If you chose to set aside 25 percent: 135.

Now you know what the month made, what is still coming and what is already spoken for.

Step 5: Do a monthly ten-minute check

  1. Match the log to your bank or payment account. Does every deposit have a row?
  2. Check that every cost has a receipt.
  3. Chase anything marked Invoiced for more than your payment terms.
  4. Look at profit. Is the hustle paying you what you hoped for the time it takes?

That last question matters. Divide monthly profit by hours spent, and you'll see your real hourly figure. It's sometimes lower than expected, and that is useful to know.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing personal and side spending. If you can, use a separate account or card.
  • Only logging when money arrives. Log invoices when you send them.
  • Skipping small costs. A few small receipts add up over a year.
  • Leaving it all until year end. Twelve small sessions beat one frantic weekend.

This is a record-keeping method, not tax or financial advice. Rules differ by country and by situation, so check your local requirements.
