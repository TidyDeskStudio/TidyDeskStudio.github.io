How to Track Photography Bookings, Deposits and Shoot Deliveries in One Sheet

A photographer's busy season is when things slip. One client has paid a deposit but you never sent the questionnaire. Another shoot happened three weeks ago and the gallery still isn't delivered. A third enquiry went quiet and you can't remember if you replied. None of that is a talent problem. It is a tracking problem, and one well-built sheet solves most of it.

Think in stages, not in dates

A booking moves through stages, and each stage has a next action. Give every client a status from a fixed list:

  1. Enquiry - someone asked, you owe a reply.
  2. Quoted - you sent prices, they owe a decision.
  3. Booked - deposit received, date held.
  4. Shot - the session is done, editing is next.
  5. Delivered - gallery sent.
  6. Paid in full - nothing left to chase.

Use data validation (a dropdown) so you never type "booked" in three different spellings. Then a filter on any status shows you exactly what needs doing.

The columns that matter

On one tab, one row per booking:

  • Client name, email, phone
  • Shoot type (family, portrait, event, product)
  • Enquiry date, shoot date, location
  • Package or price agreed
  • Deposit due, deposit received (date)
  • Balance due, balance received (date)
  • Editing deadline and delivery date
  • Status and a notes column

Resist adding thirty more. If a column is empty for most rows, it belongs in the notes.

Let formulas do the arithmetic

Balance outstanding for each row:

=C2-D2-E2

where C is the agreed price, D the deposit paid and E any later payments. A delivery deadline from the shoot date, if you promise a fixed turnaround of, say, 21 days:

=F2+21

And a flag for overdue deliveries:

=IF(AND(G2="",TODAY()>H2),"Overdue","")

where G is the delivered date and H the deadline. Add conditional formatting so an overdue row turns red. These formulas work the same in Excel and Google Sheets.

Protect your calendar with the deposit

A date isn't booked until a deposit is recorded. Keep that rule in your sheet as well as your contract: the Booked status is only allowed when the deposit-received cell has a date. It stops the awkward situation of turning down a second client for a date that the first one never confirmed.

Worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Client: Mara Ellison, family session, package price 400.
  • Deposit of 100 received on 2 November. Session on 16 November.
  • Promised turnaround: 21 days, so delivery is due 7 December.
  • After the shoot she pays 150 on the day. Balance outstanding: 400 - 100 - 150 = 150.

The sheet shows status Shot, balance 150, and a delivery due date. Before sending the gallery you can see the balance, and decide whether to release it now or on payment, as your own policy says.

A weekly five-minute review

Pick one day. Filter for Enquiry (reply to them), Quoted older than a week (a short follow-up), Shot (what is still unedited), and any balance above zero after delivery. Four filters, no guesswork.

Common mistakes

  • Tracking only the shoot date. The editing and delivery stages are where clients get anxious.
  • No record of what was promised. Note the number of images and turnaround in the row.
  • Mixing deposits into the price. Keep price, deposit and balance in separate columns.
  • Deleting lost enquiries. Mark them Lost with a reason; you will see patterns, such as price or availability.

Keep payment records as record keeping only; this isn't tax or legal advice, so check your local rules for contracts and reporting.
