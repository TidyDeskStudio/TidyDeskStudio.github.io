How to Track Payments to Contractors and Freelancers So Year-End Totals Take Minutes

You hired a designer in February, an electrician twice in spring, a bookkeeper every quarter and a courier whenever things got busy. Now the year is nearly over and someone asks a simple question: how much did you pay each of them? If the answer means scrolling through twelve months of bank statements and card exports, this guide is for you.

A payments log takes a couple of minutes per payment and turns that year-end question into a quick lookup. Here's how to set one up.

1. Start with a list of payees

Make a Payees tab with one row per contractor or vendor. Useful columns:

  • Payee - the name exactly as you'll use it everywhere else.
  • Type - Individual or Company.
  • Main service - Design, Trades, Bookkeeping, Cleaning and so on.
  • Contact email
  • Contract on file - Yes or No.
  • Details on file - Yes or No, meaning you have their contact and payment details stored safely somewhere.

Keep ID numbers and bank details out of the spreadsheet. A Yes/No column that says "we have it" is enough; the actual documents belong in a secure folder.

2. Log every payment on its own row

On a Payments tab, add one row each time money goes out:

  • Date, Payee (a dropdown from the Payees list), Invoice / ref, Description
  • Category and Method (Bank transfer, Check, Card, Cash, Payment app)
  • Amount - the fee itself
  • Expenses reimbursed - materials, mileage or other costs you paid back, kept separate from the fee
  • Invoice received - Yes or No

Then add a few helper columns. Total paid is =IF(G2="","",G2+H2). Quarter is =YEAR(A2)&" Q"&ROUNDUP(MONTH(A2)/3,0), which makes quarterly checks easy. And a Check column, =IF(I2<>"Yes","Invoice missing","Recorded"), so any payment without an invoice stands out right away.

3. Let the Payees tab total itself

Back on the Payees tab, add totals that update on their own. With the payee in A2 and the year you want in a settings cell called, say, B1:

=SUMIFS(Payments!J:J,Payments!B:B,A2,Payments!A:A,">="&DATE($B$1,1,1),Payments!A:A,"<"&DATE($B$1+1,1,1))

That's "paid this year". Copy it and change the dates for "paid last year", or drop the date conditions for "paid all time". A COUNTIFS with the same conditions gives you the number of payments.

4. Add a paperwork status

A status column pulls it together: Missing paperwork if the contract or details aren't on file, Review if the yearly total is at or above an amount you choose, otherwise Complete. The review amount is your own number, a level where you want to double-check the paperwork, not a legal threshold. Reporting rules differ by country and by type of payee, so check your local rules or ask a professional about what applies to you. This log is for record keeping, not tax or legal advice.

A worked example

Example with invented numbers. A small print shop sets its review amount at 2,000. By mid-October the Payees tab shows:

  • A freelance designer: 3 payments, 2,700 this year - status Review.
  • An electrical company: 3 payments, 2,730 including 180 of reimbursed materials - status Review.
  • A bookkeeper: 4 payments, 1,400 - Complete.
  • A cleaning company: 3 payments, 720 - Missing paperwork, because there's no signed agreement yet.

The Payments tab also shows two rows with "Invoice missing". So the owner has a clear to-do list for the week: ask two payees for invoices, get one agreement signed, and double-check the files for the two payees marked Review. All of it is done in October rather than in a January panic.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing fees and reimbursed expenses. Keep them in separate columns. You may need to see them apart later, and you can always add them together.
  • Different spellings of one payee. "Brightwire", "Brightwire Electrical" and "BW Electric" become three payees. Use a dropdown.
  • Logging from memory at year end. Add the row when you pay. It takes a minute then and an hour later.
  • Skipping the invoice. A payment without an invoice is harder to explain later. Flag it and ask while the job is fresh.
  • Not filtering by date. An all-time total isn't a yearly total. Make sure your formulas use the date range you want.

Five minutes a month

Once a month, filter the Check column for "Invoice missing", look at the Payees status column, and chase anything that's out of place. By December, the year-end totals are already sitting there.
