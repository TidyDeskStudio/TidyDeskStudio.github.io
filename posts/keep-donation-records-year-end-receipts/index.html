How to Keep Donation Records for Year-End: Cash Gifts, Goods and Missing Receipts

December arrives, you want to total up what you gave this year, and all you have is a few email confirmations, a bag-drop slip from the thrift shop and a vague memory of a sponsored fun run. Donation records fall apart because giving happens in small moments across twelve months.

A simple running log fixes that. Here is how to build one that will still make sense next December.

Start with a list of charities

Make a small tab with one row per organisation: name, cause (food and housing, health, animals, education and so on), a contact email and, if you have it, their registration number. Keeping this separate means you type each name once, and a drop-down list in your gift log prevents three spellings of the same charity.

One row per gift

On a second tab, add a row every time you give. Useful columns:

  • Gift date
  • Charity (drop-down from your list)
  • Type: Cash or Goods
  • Description (for example Monthly gift, or Two bags of winter coats)
  • Amount or estimated value
  • Receipt status: Received, Requested, Not yet asked, Not needed

Log the gift the same day if you can. It takes thirty seconds, and it is far easier than reconstructing from bank statements in December.

Goods need a fair value

For donated items, write down what you gave and a realistic second-hand value, not what you paid new. A quick way is to note the item count and a typical resale price per item, then multiply. Take a phone photo of the bag or box and drop the date into the description. Whether any of this counts for anything on a tax return depends entirely on where you live, so treat the value as your own record and check local rules.

Set a receipt threshold

You do not need a formal receipt for a coin in a collection tin. Choose an amount, say 25, above which you always want paperwork. Then add a column that flags the gaps. If the amount is in column E and the status in column F:

=IF(AND(E2>=25,F2<>"Received",F2<>"Not needed"),"Chase receipt","")

Filter that column for "Chase receipt" once a month and send a short email asking for a confirmation. Charities handle these requests all the time.

Totals you will want at year-end

With the log in place, a summary needs only a few formulas:

  • Total given: =SUM(E2:E400)
  • Cash only: =SUMIF(C2:C400,"Cash",E2:E400)
  • Goods only: =SUMIF(C2:C400,"Goods",E2:E400)
  • Gifts still missing a receipt: =COUNTIF(G2:G400,"Chase receipt")
  • Per charity: =SUMIF(B2:B400,"Food Bank Example",E2:E400)

A worked example

All invented. In a year you give 40 a month to a food charity (480), 150 to an animal shelter in spring, a 60 sponsorship for a school run, and three bags of clothes valued at 90. Total: 780. The receipt column shows the shelter gift and the sponsorship as Requested, so in early December you chase those two. Everything else is Received or Not needed. When you or your preparer asks "what did we give?", the answer takes a minute, not an afternoon.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing pledges with payments. Log the date the money actually left your account.
  • Forgetting recurring gifts. Add twelve rows in January or add them as each one hits.
  • Valuing goods at the original price. Use a realistic used value.
  • Leaving receipt requests until January. Charities are slower at year-end.

This is a record-keeping method, not tax or legal advice. Rules about what is deductible, and what proof is needed, vary by country, so ask a qualified professional or check your local tax authority.
