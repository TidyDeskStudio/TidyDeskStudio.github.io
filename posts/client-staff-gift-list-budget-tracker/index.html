How to Keep a Client and Staff Gift List So You Never Repeat a Gift (or Blow the Budget)

It is early December. You want to thank your best clients, your suppliers and the people who work with you. Then the questions start. What did you send the bookkeeper last year? Did the print shop get a card or a box? Is the gift budget 300 or 500? Nobody remembers, so you guess, and the guess usually costs more than planned.

One list, started now and kept for years, fixes this. Here is how to build it.

Step 1: List the people before you shop

Make one row per recipient. Useful columns:

  • Name and company.
  • Group: client, supplier, team, referral partner.
  • Relationship note, such as the long-standing client or the person who sorted your delivery problem.
  • Address or delivery method.
  • Last year's gift and this year's gift.

Keeping last year's gift beside this year's is the single most useful habit. It stops repeats and reminds you what landed well.

Step 2: Set a budget per group, not per person

Deciding a figure for every individual is slow. Instead, set a total for each group, then split it. For example, clients get a pool, suppliers a smaller one, team members their own. A summary formula shows how much of each pool is used:

=SUMIF(C2:C40,"Client",G2:G40)

Compare that with the group's budget cell and show the remainder:

=H2-I2

A negative number is your signal to trim before you order, not after.

Step 3: Put dates in the row

Gifts fail on logistics more than on taste. Add two date columns: order by and ship by. If you want it to arrive before a closing date, work backwards:

=K2-5

That gives a ship date five days before arrival is needed. Sort by this column each morning and the day's tasks are obvious.

Step 4: Track the thank-you card as its own step

The gift and the card are separate jobs. Add a column for card written and one for card sent, with a simple yes or blank. Then a count shows what is left:

=COUNTIF(M2:M40,"")

Step 5: Write a short note after the season

In January, add one line per recipient about the response. A reply, a thank-you call, a gift that bounced back. Next year's decisions then take minutes.

A worked example

These numbers are invented, for layout only.

  • Client pool: 400. Twelve clients at about 30 each is 360, leaving 40.
  • Supplier pool: 120. Four suppliers at 25 each is 100, leaving 20.
  • Team pool: 200. Three people at 60 is 180, leaving 20.
  • Total planned: 640 against a combined budget of 720.

Last year's column shows that two clients received the same box of treats two years running. This year they swap to something different, with no extra cost and no repeat.

Common mistakes

  • Not recording what you sent. Next year you will guess again.
  • Ordering without a ship-by date. Late December delivery is not something to leave to luck.
  • Giving the same value to everyone. Groups with different relationships can have different pools, as long as you decide it deliberately.
  • Forgetting company policies. Some organisations limit what staff can accept. When in doubt, ask or choose something modest.
  • Skipping the card. A short, specific line often matters more than the gift.

This is a planning and record-keeping method only, not tax, legal or financial advice. Check your local rules on business gifts and what you can claim.
