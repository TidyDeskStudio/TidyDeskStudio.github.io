Somewhere in a coat pocket, a kitchen drawer and a phone gallery, there are receipts for pharmacy items, glasses, dental visits and prescriptions. In December you wonder how much you have claimed, how much is still sitting in your account and whether any of those receipts count. By then you are scrambling.

If your plan has a use-it-by date, an organised receipt log turns that scramble into ten calm minutes. Here is how to build one.

Step 1: Check your plan's rules first

Plans differ: some reset on a calendar date, some give a grace period, some allow a carry-over. Find the exact deadline and the claim-submission deadline in your own plan documents and write both at the top of your sheet. Do not rely on memory or on what a friend's plan allows.

Step 2: One row per receipt

Useful columns:

  • Date of purchase
  • Who it was for (yourself, partner, child)
  • Provider or shop
  • What it was, in plain words
  • Amount
  • Claim status: not claimed, submitted, paid, rejected
  • Receipt location: photo name, folder or envelope

The receipt location column sounds fussy and saves the most time. When a claim is queried, you know where to look.

Step 3: Add up claimed and unclaimed

With status in column F and amount in column E:

=SUMIF(F2:F200,"Not claimed",E2:E200)

and

=SUMIF(F2:F200,"Paid",E2:E200)

The first number is money you have already spent on eligible-looking items but have not yet claimed. Reading it every few weeks keeps it from piling up unnoticed.

Step 4: Start a countdown

Put your use-it-by date in a cell, say B1, then show the days left:

=B1-TODAY()

Add a message beside it:

=IF(B1-TODAY()<=30,"Check balance and submit claims","")

A date you can see is a date you act on.

Step 5: Track what is left in the account

Put your annual election at the top, then subtract everything paid or submitted:

=B2-SUMIF(F2:F200,"Paid",E2:E200)-SUMIF(F2:F200,"Submitted",E2:E200)

The result is an estimate of what you still have available. Your provider's own balance is the official figure, so compare the two now and then.

A worked example

These numbers are invented. You set aside 1,000 for the year. Your log shows 640 paid, 90 submitted and 120 not yet claimed. Estimated remaining: 1,000 - 640 - 90 = 270. With 30 days to go, you submit the 120 you had forgotten, leaving about 150 to use on eligible items you were already planning to buy.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping photos with no names. "IMG_2231" tells you nothing in December. Rename with date and shop.
  • Waiting for the last week. Claim windows can be shorter than you think.
  • Mixing eligible and ineligible purchases. Add a column for "checked eligible" so you only claim what your plan covers.
  • Forgetting family members. Log who each receipt belongs to.
  • Ignoring rejected claims. Note why they were rejected so you can fix or resubmit.

This is a record-keeping method only, not medical, tax or financial advice. Check your own plan's rules and your local regulations.
