How to Track Donations and Thank-You Notes for a Small Nonprofit (Before the Year-End Rush)

If you help run a small charity, a club or a community group, you probably know the feeling: a check arrives in the mail, someone pays online, a local shop drops off a donation at the fundraiser, and three weeks later nobody can remember whether that person got a thank-you. Donor software feels like overkill for a group your size, but a loose list in a notebook isn't enough either.

The good news is that a small, well-organised spreadsheet covers almost everything a small group needs. Here is how to set one up, what to track and how to use it during the busy last months of the year.

1. Keep three lists, not one

Most donation lists go wrong because everything ends up in one long sheet. Split it into three tabs:

  • Donors - one row per person, family, business or group. Columns: Donor, Type, Email, Phone, Notes.
  • Gifts - one row per gift. Columns: Date, Donor, Amount, Method, Campaign, Gift type, Thank-you sent, Thanked by, Notes.
  • Campaigns - one row per appeal, event or fund. Columns: Campaign, Start, End, Goal.

Use a dropdown (data validation) on the Gifts tab so Donor and Campaign can only be picked from the other two lists. That one step stops "Helen K", "Helen K." and "helen" from turning into three different donors.

2. Make the thank-you impossible to forget

The thank-you is the part small groups miss most often, simply because the person who opens the mail isn't the person who writes the cards. Add a status column to the Gifts tab that works it out for you:

=IF(C2="","",IF(G2<>"","Thanked",IF(TODAY()-A2>7,"Overdue thank-you","To thank")))

Here A is the gift date, C the amount and G the date the thank-you went out. Change the 7 to whatever window your group agrees on. Then filter the column to "To thank" and "Overdue thank-you" at every meeting - that filtered list is your to-do list. The Thanked by column means anyone can see who handled it.

3. Let the Donors tab do the adding up

Once gifts are in one place, each donor's totals are simple formulas. With the donor's name in A2:

  • Lifetime giving: =SUMIFS(Gifts!C:C,Gifts!B:B,A2)
  • This year: =SUMIFS(Gifts!C:C,Gifts!B:B,A2,Gifts!A:A,">="&DATE(2026,1,1),Gifts!A:A,"<"&DATE(2027,1,1))
  • Last year: the same formula with 2025 and 2026.

Then add a status: Active if they gave this year, Gave last year if they gave last year but not yet this year, Lapsed if they gave at some point before that, and Prospect if they haven't given yet. The "Gave last year" group is the most useful list you will have in November: these are people who already care about your work and may simply not have been asked.

4. Track campaigns against a goal

On the Campaigns tab, add Raised with =SUMIFS(Gifts!C:C,Gifts!E:E,A2) and % of goal with =IF(D2=0,"",E2/D2). Keep a "General Fund" row with no dates for gifts that aren't tied to an appeal. Seeing a year-end appeal sitting at 40% in early December tells you whether you need one more email or a phone round.

A worked example

Example with invented numbers. A neighbourhood food pantry runs a Year-End Appeal from 1 October to 31 December with a goal of 6,000. By mid-November the Gifts tab shows 22 gifts adding up to 2,700, so the Campaigns tab reads 45% of goal. The Gifts tab has four rows marked "To thank" and one "Overdue thank-you" from a gift dropped off at a bake sale. The Donors tab shows nine supporters with the status "Gave last year". The volunteer coordinator sends the five thank-yous that week and writes a short personal note to the nine lapsed-this-year donors. That's a clear plan from one glance at three tabs.

Common mistakes

  • Typing names freehand. Without a dropdown, totals split across spelling variations and donor history quietly breaks.
  • Recording pledges as received. Log a pledge payment when the money actually arrives, and note "1 of 2" in Notes.
  • Putting in-kind gifts in the same pot without marking them. Use a Gift type column (One-time, Monthly, Pledge payment, In-kind, Event) so you can tell cash from goods.
  • Storing sensitive details. Keep card or bank numbers out of the sheet entirely. Name, contact and gift details are enough.
  • Waiting until January. Thank-yous sent months late feel like an afterthought. Check the status column weekly during giving season.

A note on receipts: this kind of sheet is a record of who gave what and when. It isn't tax advice and it doesn't produce official donation receipts, so check your local rules on what a receipt must include.

Keep it small and keep it up to date

Ten minutes a week is enough: add new gifts, type in thank-you dates, glance at the campaign progress. The structure does the rest.
