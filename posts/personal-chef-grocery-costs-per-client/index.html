How to Track Grocery Costs and Menus for Each Personal Chef Client

If you cook for several households, the grocery receipts pile up fast. One client is allergic to shellfish, another wants low-carb, and a third changes the menu every week. At the end of the month you are left asking: which clients actually make money? Here is a simple way to keep each client's menus and grocery spending separate, so you can answer that.

Start with one row per client

Make a client sheet with one row each. Useful columns:

  • Client name (or a code, if you prefer privacy)
  • Household size and servings per cook day
  • Allergies and foods to avoid (put this somewhere you cannot miss it)
  • Likes and dislikes
  • Cook day and delivery or storage instructions
  • Fee per cook day or per week
  • Whether groceries are billed at cost, with a handling fee, or included in your fee

Log every cook day as its own line

A cook-day log is the heart of the system. Each line has the date, client, menu, number of servings, grocery spend, your hours (shopping, cooking, cleaning, travel) and the fee charged. Keep it to the same columns every time so you can total any client with a formula:

=SUMIF(B:B,"Client Name",E:E)

where column B holds client names and column E holds grocery spend. This works in Excel and Google Sheets.

Keep menus in a reusable list

Write each dish once, with its servings and an estimated ingredient cost. When a client asks for the roast chicken week again, you can copy the menu and see what it cost last time. Over a few months you will notice which dishes are expensive for what they earn.

Match receipts to cook days

The easy trap is a shop that covers two clients. Split it at the till where you can, or write down on the receipt which items belong to whom. Photograph the receipt the same day and note the cook-day date in the file name. At month end, each client's grocery total should equal the receipts you assigned to them.

Worked example (invented numbers)

Client A pays a flat fee of 180 per cook day, groceries billed at cost. Client B pays 150 per cook day with groceries included.

  • Client B, four cook days: grocery spend 62, 71, 58, 80 = 271. Fees 600. After groceries, 329 left for 4 cook days.
  • Hours across those days: 6 + 6.5 + 5.5 + 7 = 25. So about 13.2 per hour before other costs.
  • Client A, four cook days, same hours: fees 720, groceries billed back at cost, so about 28.8 per hour.

Without the log, both clients feel the same. With it, you can see that Client B's flat fee needs raising, or the grocery allowance needs a cap.

Work out margin per client

For groceries-included clients, margin = fees minus grocery spend. Divide by your hours to get what you actually earn per hour. Look at it quarterly, not weekly, since one expensive truffle week does not tell you much.

Common mistakes

  • Not recording hours. Shopping and cleaning time are real work.
  • Mixing personal groceries with client receipts. Keep them separate from day one.
  • Losing allergy notes in a text thread. Keep them on the client row.
  • Never adjusting fees. Food prices change. Review your rates at least yearly.

This is record-keeping only, not tax or legal advice. Check local rules on food business licensing and record retention.
