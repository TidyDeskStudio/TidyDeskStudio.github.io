How to Tell If a Craft Fair Was Actually Worth It (After the Booth Fee and Everything Else)

You packed the car at six, stood behind the table all day, and came home with a cash box that felt heavy. Was it a good event? Honestly, it's hard to say. The takings are easy to count. The booth fee, fuel, parking, the lunch you bought, the card reader fees and what the stock cost you to make are not, and they all come out of that cash box.

Holiday market season is when most makers book the most events, so it's also the best time to start keeping score. Here's a simple way to judge every fair on the same terms.

1. Log every event as one row

Make an Events tab with one row per fair, market or pop-up. Useful columns:

  • Event and Date
  • Type - craft fair, farmers market, holiday market, pop-up
  • Hours - include setup and pack-down, not just opening hours
  • Cash sales and Card sales
  • Booth fee
  • Other costs - fuel, parking, food, card fees, a new tablecloth
  • Stock cost - what the items you sold cost you in materials
  • Visitors to table and Number of sales - a rough tally is fine

Fill it in that evening or the next morning, while you still remember the parking ticket.

2. Work out real profit, not takings

With cash in E, card in F, booth fee in G, other costs in H and stock cost in I:

  • Total sales: =E2+F2
  • Net profit: =E2+F2-G2-H2-I2

Stock cost is the one people skip. If you don't know it per item, start with a rough percentage of sales from your own pricing and refine it later. Leaving it out makes every event look better than it was.

3. Add the numbers that make events comparable

A two-hour evening market and a two-day festival can't be compared on profit alone. Add:

  • Profit per hour: =IF(D2=0,"",ROUND(J2/D2,2)), with hours in D and net profit in J. This is the number that tells you whether the day paid you fairly.
  • Return on fees: =IF(G2=0,"",ROUND(J2/G2,1)). How many times over the booth fee came back as profit.
  • Average sale: =IF(L2=0,"",ROUND((E2+F2)/L2,2)), with number of sales in L.
  • Conversion: number of sales divided by visitors to your table. A low figure with lots of visitors can point to pricing or display. A high figure with few visitors usually means the event was quiet.

4. Give each event a verdict

Pick an hourly profit you'd be happy with. Put it in a cell, say Settings!B1, and add a verdict column:

=IF(J2<0,"Lost money",IF(J2/D2>=Settings!B1,"Worth it","Marginal"))

Now when the next application opens, you can sort by verdict instead of relying on a vague memory of "it was busy".

A worked example

Example with invented numbers. A Saturday holiday market:

  • Sales: 480 cash and card combined, from 32 sales
  • Booth fee 90, other costs 45 (fuel, parking, lunch, card fees), stock cost 150
  • Hours: 9, including setup and pack-down

Net profit is 480 - 90 - 45 - 150 = 195. That's about 21.70 an hour, the booth fee came back a little over twice, and the average sale was 15. If your target is 20 an hour, this one is worth booking again. A smaller market the week before with 260 in sales, a 40 fee and 6 hours might look worse on takings but could still land close on profit per hour. That's exactly why you need the columns.

5. Count stock in and out

Add a Stock Count tab: Event, Item, Brought, Returned. Sold is =C2-D2 and sell-through is =IF(C2=0,"",(C2-D2)/C2). After three or four events you'll see which items sell out (bring more) and which travel home every time (bring fewer, or rethink them). That saves packing time and cash tied up in stock.

Common mistakes

  • Judging by takings. A big sales day with a big fee and a long drive can earn less than a quiet local market.
  • Leaving out your hours. Setup, pack-down and travel are part of the day. Count them.
  • Forgetting small costs. Card fees, parking and food add up over a season.
  • Deciding after one try. Weather and timing swing a single event a lot. Give a promising fair two goes before dropping it.
  • Missing apply-by dates. Popular holiday markets fill early. Add an Apply by column so the good ones don't slip past.

This is a record-keeping method, not tax advice. Check local rules for what you need to keep for your own records.

Ten minutes after each event

Count the cash box, enter the row, note what came home in the stock tub. By the end of the season you'll have a clear list of which fairs to book again and which to skip.
