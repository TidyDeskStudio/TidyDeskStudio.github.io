How to Track Volunteer Hours and Shifts Without Chasing Anyone

Small groups run on volunteers, and somebody always ends up with the job of remembering who did what. Then a funder asks for total volunteer hours, or you want to thank your most reliable helpers, and the answer lives in scattered texts and a half-filled paper sign-in sheet.

A short, consistent log solves it. Here is a layout that works for a food bank, a school club, a community garden or a local event team.

Two lists: people and shifts

Keep these separate, and link them by name or ID.

Volunteers tab with one row per person: name, contact (use a real email on your own records), start date, role or skills, availability notes, and a status (Active, On break, Left).

Shift log tab with one row per shift worked: date, volunteer, activity or team, start time, end time, hours, and a notes column. Only record shifts that actually happened; planned shifts can sit on a separate schedule tab.

Let the sheet work out hours

Type start and end times, and calculate hours so nobody does sums by hand:

=(D2-C2)*24

where C is start and D is end, both formatted as time. If a shift can run past midnight, use =MOD(D2-C2,1)*24. Format the result with one decimal place.

Total per person

On the Volunteers tab, add:

=SUMIF(ShiftLog!B:B,A2,ShiftLog!F:F)

This adds up every shift that matches the name in A2. A second column can limit it to a date range using SUMIFS, which is handy for "hours this quarter" or "hours this year".

Spot the gaps before the day

On a schedule tab, list each upcoming shift with the number of people you need and the number signed up. Then:

=IF(E2<D2,"Short "&(D2-E2),"Covered")

Shade the short ones. Sending a message on Tuesday about Saturday's shortfall works far better than finding out on Saturday morning.

A worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Ines Marlow, Tuesday pantry sort, 9:00 to 12:30 = 3.5 hours.
  • Ines Marlow, Saturday pickup run, 10:00 to 13:00 = 3.0 hours.
  • Tobias Quill, Saturday pickup run, 10:00 to 13:00 = 3.0 hours.

The totals formula gives Ines 6.5 hours and Tobias 3.0 hours for the week. If Saturday needed three people, the schedule row would show "Short 1", and you know to send one more request.

Common mistakes

  • Logging hours weeks later. Memory fades; ask volunteers to confirm at the end of each shift, or have a shift lead record it that day.
  • Inconsistent names. "Tobias Q.", "Tobias Quill" and "Toby" count as three people. Use a dropdown fed from the Volunteers tab.
  • Mixing minutes and hours. Pick decimal hours and stick to it.
  • Collecting more personal data than you need. Keep only what you use, and store it somewhere with limited access. Check the data rules that apply to your organisation and region.
  • Only counting hours, never saying thanks. A quarterly look at the totals is a natural moment to send a note to everyone who helped.

What to report

Most funders and boards want a handful of figures: total hours in the period, number of active volunteers, and hours by activity. All three come straight from SUMIFS, COUNTIF and a simple pivot table by activity. Decide which ones you need before the year ends so you record the right columns from now on.
