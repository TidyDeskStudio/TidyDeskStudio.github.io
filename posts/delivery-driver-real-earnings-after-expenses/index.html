The earnings screen at the end of a shift shows one number. It does not show the fuel you burned, the miles added to your car, the parking, or the hour you spent waiting between orders. A 90 shift can be a good day or a break-even day, and you cannot tell which without doing a little arithmetic.

This guide shows how to track each shift in a few minutes so you know what you actually keep per hour and per mile.

What to record after every shift

Keep it to one row per shift. These columns are enough:

  • Date
  • Hours logged in - from first order accepted or going online, to going offline
  • Start odometer and end odometer
  • Fares and delivery pay
  • Tips
  • Fuel or charging cost for that day
  • Other costs - parking, tolls, a hot bag, phone mount

Write the odometer numbers at the start and end, not at the end of the week. Remembering seven days of mileage is a guess. Reading a dial is a record.

Total miles, not just delivery miles

Your driving includes the trip to the first pickup, the empty miles between orders and the drive home. Most drivers who only count the loaded miles understate their car use. Log the full odometer difference with =End-Start, then, if you want to separate business from personal driving, note personal trips in their own row so they do not get mixed in.

The three numbers that tell the truth

Once you have a few shifts, add these formulas. Say earnings are in column D, tips in E, fuel in F, other costs in G, hours in B, miles in C:

  • Net for the shift: =D2+E2-F2-G2
  • Net per hour: =(D2+E2-F2-G2)/B2
  • Net per mile: =(D2+E2-F2-G2)/C2

These are before vehicle wear, insurance and tax, so treat them as a floor for what the shift cost you, not the full picture.

A worked example

Example with invented numbers.

  • Hours: 5
  • Miles: 78
  • Delivery pay: 74
  • Tips: 21
  • Fuel: 14
  • Other: 3

Net = 74 + 21 - 14 - 3 = 78. Net per hour = 78 / 5 = 15.60. Net per mile = 78 / 78 = 1.00.

The app might have shown 95 and felt like 19 an hour. After fuel and costs, it is 15.60. Not bad or good in itself, but now you can compare it with another shift.

Use the data to choose when to drive

After two or three weeks, sort or filter by day of the week and time of day. Often a short lunch window beats a long, slow evening because miles per order are lower. You may find that a day you thought was your best is mediocre once fuel is counted. Do not assume this; look at your own sheet.

Keep records tidy for tax time

Mileage and expenses are the numbers an accountant or tax form will ask for. A running log is easier to defend and much easier to total than a pile of memory. Rules differ by country and by how you are classed, so check what your local tax authority accepts. This guide is about record keeping and is not tax advice.

Common mistakes

  • Logging mileage once a week from memory.
  • Leaving out waiting time, which makes the hourly figure look better than it was.
  • Ignoring small costs like parking and tolls, which add up over a month.
  • Mixing personal and business trips in the same total.
  • Not saving a screenshot of weekly earnings to match your own sheet against.

Fill the row in as soon as you park. Thirty seconds then beats twenty minutes on Sunday.
