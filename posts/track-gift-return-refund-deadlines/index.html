It's January 9th. The sweater doesn't fit, the gadget is a duplicate, and you're pretty sure the store said something about thirty days. Was that thirty days from purchase, or from delivery? Where is the receipt? Most people lose money on returns not because stores refuse, but because the deadline passed quietly while nobody was looking.

A return log fixes this in about ten minutes of setup. Here is how to build one that actually gets used.

Why holiday returns go wrong

Three things pile up at once. Purchases are spread over many weeks and many shops. Gifts arrive in other people's hands, so you lose track of what is wrong with them. And many shops extend their return window for the holidays, which means two items bought a month apart can expire on the same day, or in the opposite order from what you'd expect.

Memory can't hold that. A single list can.

The columns you need

Keep it to one row per item. These columns cover almost everything:

  • Item - what it is, in plain words.
  • Store - where you bought it.
  • Purchase date - the date on the receipt.
  • Received date - for online orders, when it arrived. Some shops count from here.
  • Window (days) - the number from the store's policy.
  • Deadline - calculated, not typed.
  • Days left - calculated.
  • Status - Keep, Return, Exchange, Returned, Refund received.
  • Receipt location - "email folder", "kitchen drawer", "gift receipt in card".
  • Refund type - original payment, store credit, or exchange only.
  • Amount - what you expect back.

Let formulas do the date math

If purchase date is in column C and window in column E, the deadline in column F is:

=C2+E2

If the shop counts from delivery and the received date is in column D, use the later of the two bases:

=IF(D2="",C2,D2)+E2

Days left in column G:

=IF(H2="Keep","",F2-TODAY())

Where H holds the status. Blank means nothing to worry about. Add conditional formatting so anything at 7 days or fewer turns amber and anything at 0 or below turns red. Sort by Days left and the item most at risk is always at the top.

A worked example

This is invented data, just to show the idea.

  • Blue scarf, bought Nov 20, 30-day window, deadline Dec 20. It's a gift for a neighbour, who may want to swap it.
  • Kitchen scale, bought Dec 2, received Dec 6, window counts from delivery, 30 days, deadline Jan 5.
  • Board game, bought Dec 10, holiday extended window ends Jan 31.

On Dec 18 the sheet shows the scarf at 2 days left in amber. That is your nudge to ask the neighbour this weekend rather than discover it in January. The scale deadline is later even though it was bought later than the scarf only by a few days, a thing you would easily get backwards in your head.

Record the policy, not just the date

Add a short notes column for the odd rules: "unopened only", "restocking fee", "gift receipt required", "exchange or credit only". Copy this from the receipt or the shop's page at the time of purchase, because policies change and the version that applied on your purchase day is the one that matters. If you aren't sure, ask the shop and write down the answer with the date.

Handle gifts you give

If you buy a gift, also log who it's for and whether you included a gift receipt. It stops the awkward moment where someone asks to exchange something and you can't remember where it came from. Use the Status column to mark "Gift given - receipt included" so you know the item is out of your hands but the deadline still exists if they ask.

Close the loop on refunds

A return isn't finished when you hand the item over. Mark it Returned, then watch for the refund. Many refunds take several days to a couple of weeks to appear. Add a "Refund expected by" column and check it once a week. If the money hasn't arrived, you have the date, store and amount ready for a quick message.

Common mistakes

  • Typing the deadline by hand. One typo and the whole system is wrong. Calculate it.
  • Forgetting the clock may start at delivery. Or at purchase. Check which, per shop.
  • Tossing packaging early. Keep boxes until the window closes.
  • Only logging things you plan to return. The item you think you'll keep is the one that turns out to be faulty in week three.
  • Skipping the receipt location. A deadline is useless without proof of purchase.

Set a weekly check

Pick one day, say Sunday, and open the sheet for two minutes. Sort by Days left, deal with anything under a week, and close it. That small habit is the whole system.
