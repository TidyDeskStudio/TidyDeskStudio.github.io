How to Plan a December Family Calendar and Holiday Budget Without Double-Booking Everyone

December arrives with a school concert, two parties, a baking day, an office do and a pile of gifts, all in about four weeks. Half of it lives in your head, a third in group chats, and the rest on a fridge calendar nobody updated. Then two things land on the same Thursday and the card balance is a surprise in January.

One calendar and one budget, both kept in the same place, solve most of this. Here is a simple way to set them up in a spreadsheet.

Step 1: Dump every event in one list

Before you plan anything, write down everything that is already happening or likely to. Ask each person in the house what is on their radar. Use one row per event with these columns:

  • Date and start time.
  • Event (concert, party, baking day, visit).
  • Who it involves.
  • Cost you expect, even if it is only a rough guess.
  • Needs (a gift, a dish to bring, a costume, travel).

Sort by date and you already have a calendar you can read at a glance.

Step 2: Find the clashes automatically

Reading down the list works until December gets busy. Add a helper column that counts how many events share a date:

=COUNTIF(A2:A60,A2)

Anything above 1 is a day to look at. Add a second column that turns it into a flag:

=IF(COUNTIF(A2:A60,A2)>1,"CHECK","")

A clash is not always a problem, since a school concert at 4pm and a party at 7pm can both happen, but you will want to decide that on purpose rather than discover it on the day.

Step 3: Give every gift a line

Gifts are where the budget usually leaks. Make a second table with a row per person: name, gift idea, planned spend, actual spend, bought (yes or no), wrapped (yes or no). Include the small ones, such as teachers, neighbours and the postal worker, because small amounts add up quickly.

Step 4: Split the budget into lines

One big number is hard to manage. Break your December total into lines you can watch separately:

  • Gifts
  • Food and drink
  • Events and outings
  • Travel
  • Decorations and extras

For each line, record a budget and an actual amount, then show what is left:

=B2-C2

Add the actuals with =SUM(C2:C6) and compare them with the total you set. If one line is running hot in week one, you can trim another before it hurts.

Step 5: Use the countdown days

If you do an advent-style countdown, treat each day as a small slot in the same sheet: a tiny activity, a note, a treat. Filling those in ahead of time stops the 11pm scramble on the first of the month, and it lets you see which days are already packed so you give those a very light activity.

A worked example

All numbers are invented, for layout only.

  • Total December budget: 900. Gifts 400, food 200, events 120, travel 100, extras 80.
  • By 10 December you have spent 210 on gifts, 60 on food, 45 on events, 0 on travel and 30 on extras, so 345 in total.
  • Gifts: 400 - 210 = 190 left, with five names still unbought.
  • Calendar check: the 19th has a concert, a bake sale and a visit from relatives. The clash flag shows CHECK, so you move the bake sale prep to the 18th.

None of that needed anything clever, just one place where it all lives.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping separate lists per person. Clashes only show when everything is together.
  • Forgetting small costs. Cards, postage, wrapping and parking are easy to leave out.
  • Budgeting gifts but not food. Hosting costs often match the gift total.
  • Setting the budget on 24 December. Decide before you spend, not after.
  • Never recording actuals. A budget with no actuals is only a wish.

This is a family planning and record-keeping method, not financial advice. Adjust the numbers to your own situation.
