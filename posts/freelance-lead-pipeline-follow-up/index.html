How to Keep Track of Freelance Leads So Nobody Goes Quiet on You

Most freelancers don't lose work because they were turned down. They lose it because a promising conversation got buried under deadlines and nobody followed up. A lead who said "send me a quote next week" and heard nothing for a month has usually moved on.

You don't need a heavy sales system. A single sheet with the right columns will keep every lead visible.

Set up one row per opportunity

One row is one possible job, not one person. A returning client with two projects gets two rows. Columns that earn their place:

  • Lead name and company
  • Source (referral, website, past client, social)
  • Service they asked about
  • Stage (see below)
  • Estimated value
  • Last contact date
  • Next action and Next action date
  • Notes

Keep the stages few and clear

Five is plenty: New, Talking, Quote sent, Won, Lost. More stages look tidy and then nobody updates them. Use a dropdown (Data validation in Excel, Data > Data validation in Google Sheets) so the spelling is always identical, which matters for the formulas below.

Always write the next action

This is the habit that makes the sheet work. Every open lead needs a next step and a date: "Send three sample links, Friday" or "Ask about budget, 3 Nov". A lead with an empty next-action cell is a lead you are about to forget.

Make the sheet tell you who is overdue

Add a column called Days since contact:

=IF(F2="","",TODAY()-F2)

(where column F holds the last contact date). Then add a status column:

=IF(OR(D2="Won",D2="Lost"),"",IF(H2<TODAY(),"Overdue","OK"))

(where column D is the stage and column H is the next action date). Add a conditional-format rule that turns "Overdue" red and sort by that column each morning. Five minutes of looking at red rows beats an hour of wondering who you forgot.

See the value of what is in play

On a summary area, total the estimated value per stage:

=SUMIF(D:D,"Quote sent",E:E)

Count of leads per stage uses COUNTIF the same way. Seeing the money sitting in "Quote sent" is a good prompt to follow up on those first.

A worked example

Invented names and numbers.

  • Harbour Bakes, website redesign, stage Quote sent, value 1,800, last contact 12 Oct, next action "Check they received the quote" on 19 Oct.
  • Dana Orchard, logo refresh, stage Talking, value 600, next action "Send moodboard" on 24 Oct.
  • Fernwood Studio, monthly newsletters, stage New, value 400 a month, next action "Reply to enquiry" today.

On 22 Oct the first row shows Overdue, because the check-in date has passed. That is the message to send first. The other two are fine until their dates.

Common mistakes

  • Tracking only wins. Log lost leads with a reason; patterns show up fast (price, timing, scope).
  • Vague next actions. "Follow up" is not an action; "Email revised scope" is.
  • Updating in batches. Log the contact right after it happens or the dates drift.
  • Too many columns. If you won't fill it in, delete it.
  • Never reviewing. Pick a fixed time, such as Monday morning, to scan the whole list.

A gentle follow-up rhythm

A short message a few days after sending a quote is normal and polite. Something like: "Just checking the quote reached you, happy to adjust the scope if that helps." If there is still no answer, one more nudge a week or two later is plenty, then mark the lead Lost with the note "no reply" and move on. You can always reopen it.
