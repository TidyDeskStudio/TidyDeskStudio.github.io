How to Set Reorder Points So You Stop Running Out of Your Best Sellers

It always seems to be the popular item. A customer asks for it, you check the shelf, and the last one went yesterday. Meanwhile the back room holds three boxes of something that hasn't sold since summer. Both problems cost money, and both come from the same gap: nobody knows the real stock count until it's too late.

The fix doesn't need inventory software. A stock log, a running total and one number per item, the reorder point, will catch most shortfalls before they happen. With the holiday rush coming, now is a good time to set it up.

1. Keep one item list

Make an Items tab with a row per product:

  • SKU - a short code, like CND-LAV-8
  • Item and Category
  • Supplier
  • Unit cost
  • Lead time (days) - how long from placing an order to having it on the shelf
  • Reorder point - you'll work this out below

Lead time is the column most people guess at. Look back at your last two or three orders and use the real number, including any time it takes you to actually place the order.

2. Log stock movements, not just counts

Rather than overwriting a stock number, keep a Stock Log with a row every time stock moves: Date, SKU, Type (Stock in, Sale, Return, Write-off), Qty.

Type every quantity as a positive number and let the formula decide the direction. On the Items tab, current stock is:

=SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Stock in")+SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Return")-SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Sale")-SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Write-off")

If you sell online and in person, you can log a day's sales as one row per item instead of one per sale.

3. Work out how fast each item sells

Average daily sales over the last 30 days, with today's date in Settings!B1:

=SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Sale",Log!A:A,">="&(Settings!B1-30))/30

From that, days of stock left is current stock divided by daily sales, wrapped in IFERROR so items with no recent sales don't show an error. If days left is shorter than the lead time, you're already late.

4. Set the reorder point

The reorder point is the stock level at which you should place an order. A simple version:

Reorder point = daily sales x lead time + a buffer

The buffer covers a busier week or a slow delivery. Many small shops start with a buffer of a week or so of sales and adjust once they see how often they still run low. In the sheet, with daily sales in H and lead time in F, and buffer days in Settings!B2:

=ROUNDUP(H2*(F2+Settings!B2),0)

Then a status column does the watching for you:

=IF(G2<=0,"Out of stock",IF(G2<=I2,"Reorder now",IF(G2<=I2*1.5,"Reorder soon","OK")))

where G is current stock and I is the reorder point.

A worked example

Example with invented numbers. A small gift shop sells about 3 lavender candles a day. The supplier takes 10 days to deliver, and the owner wants a 5-day buffer. The reorder point is 3 x (10 + 5) = 45.

On Monday the shelf and back room hold 52. Status: Reorder soon. By Thursday, after a busy weekend market, it's 41, under 45, so the sheet says Reorder now. An order placed that day arrives with roughly 15 candles still in stock, instead of the shop running dry in the middle of December.

5. Spot slow movers too

Add a column for the date of the last sale, using a formula that also works in older spreadsheet versions: =SUMPRODUCT(MAX((Log!B2:B2000=A2)*(Log!C2:C2000="Sale")*Log!A2:A2000)). Anything with no sale in, say, 90 days is a slow mover. Those are candidates for a bundle, a discount table or simply not reordering. It frees up cash and shelf space for things that sell.

Common mistakes

  • Overwriting the count. Without a log you can't see where stock went or catch errors.
  • Ignoring write-offs. Damaged, expired or sample stock that isn't logged leaves the sheet showing more than you have.
  • Using one reorder point for everything. Fast sellers and slow sellers need different levels.
  • Forgetting seasonal swings. Before a busy season, look at last year's pace, not last month's.
  • Skipping physical counts. Count the shelf every month or two and log a correction so the sheet stays true.

Five minutes a day

Log what came in and what sold, glance at the Reorder now list, place the orders. It quickly becomes routine, and running out of your best sellers becomes rare.
