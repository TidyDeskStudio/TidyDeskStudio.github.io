A phone call comes in, you fix the unit, you invoice it. Three months later the same unit is back, and you can't remember what you did last time. Meanwhile a customer on your maintenance plan quietly passes their renewal date and nobody calls them.

Neither problem is hard to fix. You just need a record that is organised around the unit and the customer, not around the day's work orders. Here's a layout that works in Excel or Google Sheets.

Use three tabs, not one long list

One list of calls gets messy fast. Split the information so each fact is written down once:

  • Customers - name, phone, address, notes, whether they have a plan.
  • Units - one row per system or appliance: type, brand, model, install year, location, customer.
  • Calls - one row per visit: date, unit, problem, work done, parts, labour hours, amount charged, follow-up needed.

Give each unit a short ID such as U-014. Typing that ID on every call lets you pull up the full history of a unit in seconds.

Columns for the Calls tab

  • Call date
  • Customer
  • Unit ID
  • Reported problem
  • Cause found
  • Work done
  • Parts cost and labour hours
  • Amount charged
  • Follow-up needed? (Yes or No) and follow-up date

"Cause found" is the column most people skip, and it is the useful one. When you can sort by cause, patterns appear.

Spot repeat repairs

Count how many calls each unit has had. With unit IDs in column C of the Calls tab:

=COUNTIF(Calls!C:C,A2)

Put this next to each unit on the Units tab. Any unit with three or more calls in a year is worth a conversation: repair, replace or a proper inspection. It is also a conversation that can lead to a bigger job.

Track maintenance plans

For each plan customer, add these columns to the Customers tab: plan start date, plan renewal date, visits included, visits done.

Days until renewal:

=C2-TODAY()

A flag that appears 30 days before:

=IF(AND(C2-TODAY()<=30,C2-TODAY()>=0),"Call to renew",IF(C2<TODAY(),"Lapsed",""))

Visits still owed:

=D2-E2

Sort by the flag once a week. Customers marked "Lapsed" are the easiest ones to win back because they already know you.

A worked example

This is invented data, just to show the idea.

  • Customer: Alder Court Flats (invented). Unit U-014, a rooftop unit installed in 2014.
  • Calls this year: 4 (low airflow, tripped breaker, low airflow, noisy fan).
  • Plan renewal date: 20 December, visits included 2, visits done 1.

The sheet shows four calls, two with the same cause, and a plan that renews soon with a visit still owed. That is a clear reason to book the second visit and to talk about the unit's condition before renewal.

What to do each week

  1. Enter every call the same day, while you remember the cause.
  2. Filter Calls for "Follow-up needed = Yes" and ring those customers.
  3. Filter Customers for "Call to renew" and "Lapsed".
  4. Check the repeat-call count on the Units tab.

Common mistakes

  • Logging by customer only. Customers can have several units, and the history belongs to the unit.
  • Leaving out parts and hours. Without them you can't see which jobs make money.
  • Typing the same customer name differently. Use a drop-down list so counts stay accurate.
  • Waiting until the end of the week to enter calls. Details fade quickly.

This is a record-keeping tool, not safety or regulatory advice. Follow your local licensing and safety rules for the work itself.
