How to Plan a Home Move: A Week-by-Week Checklist and Budget

Moving is a hundred small jobs with a hard deadline attached. The stress usually is not the boxes. It is the feeling that you have forgotten something, and the bills that appear one after another. Two lists fix most of that: a timeline of tasks and a budget of costs. Here is how to set both up.

Start with a timeline counted backwards

Pick your moving day and count back from it. Give each task a due date, an owner if more than one person is helping, and a status column with Not started, In progress or Done.

Six to eight weeks before

  • Set the budget (see below)
  • Request quotes from movers, or book a van if you will drive yourself
  • Check your lease or contract for notice requirements
  • Start sorting. Sell, donate or throw away what you will not take

Three to four weeks before

  • Book the mover or van, and confirm the date in writing
  • Collect boxes, tape, markers and padding
  • Pack the things you use rarely: books, off-season clothes, decorations
  • Plan the utilities: end dates at the old place, start dates at the new one

One to two weeks before

  • Update your address with your bank, employer, insurers, schools and subscriptions
  • Set up mail forwarding
  • Pack a first-night box with chargers, toiletries, bedding, basic tools and snacks
  • Confirm the time and access details with the mover or building manager

Moving week

  • Photograph meter readings at both addresses
  • Defrost the freezer and empty the fridge
  • Keep valuables and important papers with you, not in the van
  • Walk through the empty old home and take photos before handing over keys

Build the budget in three groups

Make a sheet with columns for Item, Estimate, Actual and Difference. The Difference column is =C2-B2, so a positive number means you went over. Add a total row with =SUM(B2:B30) under each column.

Group the rows so nothing slips through:

  • Moving itself: movers or van hire, fuel, packing supplies, storage, tips
  • Old home: cleaning, repairs, final utility bills, any deposit adjustments
  • New home: deposit, first month, connection fees, new locks, basic furniture, curtains, cleaning products

A worked example

These figures are made up to show the layout.

  • Van hire: estimate 180, actual 195, difference +15
  • Boxes and tape: estimate 60, actual 48, difference -12
  • Final clean: estimate 120, actual 120, difference 0
  • Internet connection: estimate 50, actual 65, difference +15

Estimates total 410, actuals total 428, so you are 18 over. That is useful to know on day one, not after the last receipt. Add a line at the bottom called Surprises, and put 10 percent of your estimate in it before you start. Something always turns up.

The costs people forget

  • Overlapping rent or mortgage for a week or two
  • Takeaway meals when the kitchen is packed
  • Parking permits or a truck loading permit
  • Replacing items that did not survive the move
  • Time off work, if you lose pay for it

Common mistakes

  • Leaving utilities until the last week. Some providers need several days.
  • Not labelling by room. Write the destination room, not the contents, on every box side.
  • Packing the kettle first. Pack the essentials last.
  • Keeping receipts in a bag. Log each cost as it happens.
