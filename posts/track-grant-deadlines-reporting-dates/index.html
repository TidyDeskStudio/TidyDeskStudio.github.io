How to Track Grant Deadlines and Reporting Dates So Nothing Slips

Grants arrive in waves. One funder wants an application in March, another wants a mid-year report in July, a third wants a final report and a receipts summary a year after the money lands. When those dates live in different emails and one person's memory, something eventually gets missed.

One sheet with every grant on it fixes that. Here is a layout a small nonprofit, school group or community project can set up in an afternoon.

One row per grant, one row per deadline

The common mistake is putting everything on a single row. A single grant can have an application date, a decision date, an interim report and a final report. Use two tabs:

  • Grants tab: funder, programme name, status (Researching, Drafting, Submitted, Awarded, Declined, Closed), amount requested, amount awarded, award date, grant period start and end, and the contact person at the funder.
  • Deadlines tab: grant name, what is due (application, interim report, final report, receipt summary), due date, owner on your team, status, and a notes column.

Link the two by grant name. Use a dropdown on the Deadlines tab so spelling stays identical.

Make the sheet tell you what is coming

Add a column called Days Left:

=IF(F2="Done","",E2-TODAY())

where E is the due date and F is the status. Then add a flag column:

=IF(F2="Done","",IF(E2-TODAY()<0,"OVERDUE",IF(E2-TODAY()<=14,"Due soon","")))

Shade OVERDUE red and Due soon amber with conditional formatting. Sort by due date each Monday and the next two weeks are obvious.

Add internal deadlines, not just funder deadlines

A report due on the 30th is really due on the 20th, because someone has to pull figures, someone has to read it, and someone has to submit it. Add a column called Internal Draft Date and set it with a formula:

=E2-10

Change the 10 to whatever your team really needs. Track the internal date as the working deadline and the funder date as the hard stop.

Track what you promised

Awards usually come with conditions: spend on certain categories, hit a number of participants, credit the funder, return unspent funds. Add a third small tab called Conditions with grant name, condition in plain words, how you will show it, and who collects the evidence. When report time comes, you are not rereading a forty-page agreement to remember what you agreed to.

Worked example

Invented funder and numbers.

  • Grant: Harbour Lane Community Fund, youth reading club, awarded 6,000.
  • Interim report due 15 June; internal draft date 5 June.
  • Final report due 15 December; internal draft date 5 December.
  • Condition: at least 20 children attend; evidence is the sign-in sheet totals.

On 3 June the sheet shows the interim report as Due soon, and the Conditions tab reminds you to bring the attendance totals.

Common mistakes

  • Tracking only applications. Reports are where renewal chances are made or lost.
  • Leaving declined grants off. Keep them with the reason, if you were told. It stops you reapplying to the wrong programme.
  • No owner on each deadline. A deadline everyone can see and nobody owns is the one that gets missed.
  • Forgetting the grant period. Money often has to be spent between two dates; record both.
  • Not recording the funder's portal login location. Note where credentials are kept (never the password itself), so a colleague can submit if you are away.

A weekly five-minute check

Pick a day, open the Deadlines tab, sort by due date, and read the top ten rows. Update statuses, assign anything unowned, and move on. This habit matters more than any formula.
