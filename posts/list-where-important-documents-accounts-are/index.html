How to Make a List of Where Your Important Documents and Accounts Are

Ask yourself a simple question: if you were unwell tomorrow, could someone else find your insurance policy, the deed or tenancy agreement, your bank details and the number for your accountant? For most households the honest answer is "eventually, after a lot of digging".

A locator list fixes that. It doesn't hold the documents or any secrets. It just says what exists, where it is, and who to call. Here's how to build one in an evening.

What goes on the list

Work through these groups and add a row for each item you actually have:

  • Identity - passports, birth certificates, driving licences.
  • Home - deed, mortgage or tenancy agreement, home insurance.
  • Money - bank accounts, savings, pensions, investment accounts, loans, credit cards.
  • Insurance - car, life, health, pet, travel.
  • Legal - will, power of attorney, if you have them.
  • Work and tax - employer contact, accountant, recent tax records.
  • Digital - email accounts, phone plan, subscriptions, cloud storage.
  • Contacts - lawyer, accountant, insurance broker, doctor, landlord.

The columns

  • Item - "Home insurance policy".
  • Organisation - the company or office.
  • Reference - a policy or account reference, only the last four digits for anything financial.
  • Where the paperwork is - "Blue folder, top drawer" or "Scanned, in the folder called Home".
  • Who to call - a phone number or email.
  • Renewal or review date - if it has one.
  • Last checked - the date you last confirmed the row is right.

The one rule: no passwords

Don't write passwords, PINs, security answers or full account numbers in the sheet. Anyone who finds the file would have a lot more than they need. Instead write where the credentials are kept, for example "in my password manager", and let the right person be set up with access separately.

Add a simple review reminder

With the Last checked date in column G, this formula flags rows that haven't been looked at for a year:

=IF(TODAY()-G2>365,"Review","")

Once a year, sort by that column, fix what has changed, and update the dates.

A worked example

All names and values here are invented.

  • Item: Home insurance. Organisation: Harbor Mutual (invented). Reference: ending 4471. Paperwork: blue folder, top drawer. Who to call: the number on the policy. Renewal: 3 March.
  • Item: Savings account. Organisation: Lakeside Credit Union (invented). Reference: ending 0092. Paperwork: online statements only. Who to call: branch number.

Two rows, and already someone could find the policy and know which account to ask about.

Where to keep and share it

  1. Save a copy somewhere you can reach it, and print one for the folder where you keep paperwork.
  2. Tell one trusted person that it exists and where it is.
  3. Update it when something big changes: a move, a new job, a new policy.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to scan and organise everything first. Make the list first; tidy the folders later.
  • Leaving out digital accounts. They are often the hardest to track down.
  • Never telling anyone. A list nobody knows about helps nobody.
  • Letting it go stale. Use the review flag.

This is a record-keeping tool, not legal, financial or estate advice. Ask a qualified professional about wills and other legal documents in your area.
