How to Find Every Subscription You're Paying For (and Decide Which to Cancel)

Subscriptions are easy to start and easy to forget. A free trial you meant to cancel, a yearly renewal that lands once and then vanishes from memory, an app the kids signed up for. None of them feel big on their own. The problem is that nobody ever sees them added up.

This guide walks through finding them all, putting them on one list and deciding what stays. It takes about an hour the first time and a few minutes a month after that.

This is about keeping a clear record of what you pay. It isn't financial advice.

1. Go hunting for recurring charges

Open the last three months of statements for every card and bank account you use, plus any online payment accounts. Three months matters because quarterly charges only show up once in that window. For yearly ones, search your email for words like "renewal", "receipt", "your plan" and "trial" over the past twelve months.

Also check the subscription settings on your phone's app store and on any streaming or gaming devices at home. Those are the ones people miss most.

2. Give each one a row

Make a table with these columns:

  • Service - a name you'll recognise
  • Category - streaming, software, news, fitness, food, cloud storage, memberships
  • Price - what each bill actually charges
  • Cycle - Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly or Yearly (a dropdown keeps it tidy)
  • Next renewal - the date of the next charge
  • Trial ends - only for free trials
  • Paid with - which card or account
  • How often used - Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Rarely
  • Decision - Keep, Review or Cancel

3. Turn every bill into a monthly cost

Comparing a weekly bill with a yearly one is hard. Convert everything to the same unit. With price in C and cycle in D:

=IF(D2="Weekly",C2*52/12,IF(D2="Monthly",C2,IF(D2="Quarterly",C2/3,IF(D2="Yearly",C2/12,""))))

Then a yearly cost column is just the monthly cost times 12. Total both columns at the bottom. The yearly total is usually the number that gets people's attention.

4. Get a warning before renewals and trials

Pick a warning window, say 7 days, and put it in a cell like $M$1. With next renewal in E and trial end in F:

=IF(AND(F2<>"",F2-TODAY()<=$M$1,F2>=TODAY()),"Trial ending",IF(AND(E2<>"",E2-TODAY()<=$M$1,E2>=TODAY()),"Renews soon",IF(AND(E2<>"",E2<TODAY()),"Update date","")))

"Update date" means the renewal has passed and you need to roll the date forward. Add conditional formatting so anything flagged turns orange, and you'll spot it each time you open the file.

5. Decide what stays

Sort by monthly cost, biggest first, and look at the How often used column next to it. Anything you pay a lot for and use rarely goes to Review. Some useful questions:

  • Would I sign up for this again today at this price?
  • Do two services overlap, like two music or two cloud storage plans?
  • Is there a cheaper plan level or a shared household plan?
  • Could I pause it for the months I don't use it?

When you cancel something, keep the row, set Decision to Cancel and note the date. A "Saved per year" column (yearly cost when Decision is Cancel, otherwise 0) shows what the clear-out was worth.

A worked example (invented numbers)

Someone finds 11 recurring charges. Three are weekly or quarterly, one is yearly at 89, and the rest are monthly. Converted, they come to 142 a month, or 1,704 a year. Two video services are both marked Rarely, a fitness app hasn't been opened since spring, and a photo editing trial is due to bill yearly in five days. Cancelling those four saves 46 a month, or 552 a year, and the trial is stopped before it charges.

Common mistakes

  • Only checking one card. Subscriptions hide on old cards and payment accounts.
  • Forgetting yearly renewals because they didn't show in last month's statement.
  • Deleting cancelled rows. Keep them, so you can check the charge actually stopped.
  • Never updating dates. Roll the next renewal forward after each charge, or the warnings stop working.
